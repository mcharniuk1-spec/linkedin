A feature list can tell a buyer what your product does. It rarely tells them why the product works this way.

Take an approval step in an AI workflow. A buyer may want to know whether a person can review an uncertain result before it affects the next action. The useful story is the actual choice behind that step: what the team needed to protect, what other route it considered, and what trade-off came with the decision.

That reasoning helps a reader judge whether the product fits the work they need to do. It also gives a founder something specific to explain, instead of another round of broad claims about innovation or efficiency.

At Outspoken, we help founders bring this reasoning into view through interviews, original writing and a founder review of the facts and voice. The approval-step example is illustrative.

Try this with one feature: write down the buyer's question, the real decision behind it, and the evidence that supports your explanation. Leave any missing answer open.
