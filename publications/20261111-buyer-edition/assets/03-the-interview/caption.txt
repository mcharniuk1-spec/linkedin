“Tell me about your product” is a wide-open prompt. It can produce a polished description and still miss the part a buyer needs to understand: how the team made a choice.

A more focused interview starts with a real decision. What was the team trying to make possible? What constraint mattered? Which other option was genuinely on the table? What information did the team have at the time?

Those questions do not require a dramatic launch story. They help a founder explain ordinary, consequential judgment: why a workflow asks for a review, why an integration came first, or why a product leaves a step in the user's hands. Those are illustrative prompts, not claims about a specific company.

Then keep the timeline honest. The choice is one fact; what happened afterward is another; the founder's explanation today may include hindsight. A good draft does not flatten them into “we knew this would work.”

At Outspoken, we use interviews to find the decision and original writing to make its reasoning clear. The founder reviews the details and voice before sharing.

For your next draft, pick one decision and collect the goal, constraint, plausible alternative and confirmed consequence. If a part is unknown, leave it out until you can verify it.
