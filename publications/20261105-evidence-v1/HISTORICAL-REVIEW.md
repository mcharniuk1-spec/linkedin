# Historical LinkedIn research: 100-post excerpt review

**This is a fallback analysis, not the requested 100 newly collected posts.** The current LinkedIn source is blocked. This review uses a frozen existing private packet and does not make new LinkedIn requests. None of the 100 records establishes a current trend, conversion result, or representative LinkedIn benchmark.

## What was reviewed

From 153 previously captured pending records, I identified 113 distinct records with a source publication time, an observed reaction count, text and a source-recorded author URL, captured between 7 and 30 days after publication. I selected the 100 with the highest observed reactions in that broad age band. I checked for duplicate activity IDs, underlying share IDs, canonical URLs and normalized text. The selected rows contain 63 distinct source-recorded author URLs. Those identities were not independently verified in this review.

I inspected the opening and closing text excerpts of all 100 records and assigned each a theme, content category and relevance hypothesis. **This is an excerpt review.** I did not inspect the complete text of every record, verify the authors' claims, inspect images, watch videos, verify transcripts, or independently confirm source completeness. The owner’s private item-level CSV (not included in this public projection) preserves record IDs, source and author links, dates, separately recorded counters, categories and limitations. It contains no copied post text or media.

The posts were published from **2–28 October 2026**; their selected captures occurred from **31 October–4 November 2026**. Publication-to-capture age was 7–30 days, still a wide range for comparison. These are historical single observations. They are not a count of posts collected today, and the 63 observed URLs are not proof of 63 verified top-performing accounts.

The 45 unavailable comment counts are **unknown**, not zero. Reactions and comments have different denominators. The records include 56 source-classified Person posts and 44 Organization posts; one author accounts for nine posts. No post has two compatible observations in this review, so there is no measured velocity. Without reach, audience size, conversions or reliable equal-age observations, these counts cannot show which approach caused business results.

The two largest observed reaction counts belong to posts that my excerpt-based review classified as low fit for Outspoken's immediate buyer. That is a useful caution when selecting examples: absolute engagement alone does not establish relevance to B2B AI SaaS founders. It does not establish that relevance reduces engagement either. The sample was deliberately selected by reactions and is not representative.

## Practical ideas for Outspoken

1. **Turn founder judgment into buyer context.** Ask which alternative the founder rejected, what evidence mattered, and what the buyer should understand before evaluating the product. Use a real, cleared decision rather than inventing a founder story.
2. **Demonstrate differentiation.** Show a generic claim, explain why it could fit several competitors, then replace it with a specific buyer, situation and supported difference. A carousel could take one step per slide.
3. **Translate a feature into a decision.** Map the trigger, information used, AI output, human check and next action. Confirm the real workflow with the founder and mark any hypothetical example as illustrative.
4. **Build proof into the workflow.** Present a claim next to its source, scope and limitations. Separate a client's actual outcome from an editorial hypothesis or outside survey. No sample reactions establish sales impact.
5. **Give founder and company profiles distinct jobs.** Test a founder explanation of a decision alongside a company-page workflow or product evidence post. This is an editorial experiment, not a proven channel-performance rule.
6. **Use customer objections as prompts.** Record the buyer's exact question with permission, explain the relevant trade-off, and develop one concise post or visual around it. A buyer objection is not evidence of purchase intent.

The strongest recurring editorial opportunity in the sampled excerpts is to make the founder's reasoning, buyer situation and evidence easier to understand. The observed metrics support describing this selected sample only; they do not prove that any format, tactic or topic will outperform another.
