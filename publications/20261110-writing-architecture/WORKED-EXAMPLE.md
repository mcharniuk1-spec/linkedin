# A filled example: evidence-led LinkedIn post

Pattern: **P01 — Evidence-led insight**. Source concept: `02-what-we-actually-saw`. Status: proposed caption for founder review. The example uses existing project data; it does not invent a founder memory, customer or business result.

## Brief

**Reader:** a founder considering how reference research should inform their LinkedIn content.

**Reader question:** what can a reaction median from a selected sample tell us about what to write?

**Point:** use the sample to formulate writing questions and hypotheses, while keeping its measurement limits visible.

**Evidence:** [the preserved report data](../../reports/20261105/data.json) contains 279 post rows and 61 author rows. Reaction counts are available for 276 posts, with a median of 107.5. These counts and the median were recomputed for this update. The source observations are historical; see [the dated report](../../reports/20261105/README.md).

**Reasoning:** the statistic describes the observed sample. Differences between authors, topics and post ages prevent its use as a universal target. Without reach or conversion data, it cannot identify either outcome.

**Hook promise:** explain what the number means and give a useful way to act on the research.

## Proposed caption

Our reference sample has a median of 107.5 reactions. What can we actually learn from it?

That figure comes from the 276 posts with an observed reaction count, within a selected set of 279 posts from 61 accounts. The accounts, topics and post ages differ.

We did not observe reach, clicks or sales. The median describes those recorded reactions; it cannot tell a new account what to expect or show that a particular format caused a business result.

I would use the research to ask more specific writing questions. What decision does a post explain? What material supports its claim? Where does the explanation make a useful distinction for the intended buyer?

Those questions give us structures to consider for original work. The next step is to choose a question worth testing and keep the supporting evidence and its limits attached.

Historical reference collection: 20–22 October 2026. The metrics used in this report were recorded on 22 October. Reactions combine LinkedIn reaction types; comments are a separate measure.

Source: https://github.com/mcharniuk1-spec/linkedin/tree/main/reports/20261105

## Why the structure fits

| Functional block | What it contributes | What would break it |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Names the observed number and the interpretive question. | Presenting the number as an expected result for the reader. |
| Evidence | Supplies the observed denominator and overall sample. | Using 279 as the reaction denominator. |
| Reasoning and limit | Explains which inference is unavailable and why. | Jumping from descriptive differences to a causal format rule. |
| Application | Turns the research into specific questions about composition. | Claiming the original full posts were reread in this update. |
| Conclusion | Closes the opening with a bounded use of the research. | Adding a reach guarantee or an unrelated service pitch. |
| Source note | Keeps the historical period and metric meaning available. | Making the old sample appear to describe current platform trends. |

The body contains questions as analytical criteria. It does not finish with a request for comments. A later version could invite a concrete methodological objection, but that is optional. No humor is needed for this particular numerical explanation.

The first-person interpretation is proposed author voice and still needs founder approval. Its data does not need to become more dramatic to support the point. If the caption is adapted to a carousel, the denominator, period and limit must travel with the displayed number.
