## How to use the update

Start from the mapped structure rather than pasting its headings into a caption. Draft only with the source material actually available. A placeholder for a founder decision stays a placeholder until an approved interview supplies it. Humor examples are optional original illustrations, not facts about the founders.

Post 02 remains the research-observation post. Post 09 becomes a question-to-measure diagnostic so the pair no longer relies on repeating the same medians. Post 05 uses the explicitly hypothetical workflow from evidence-v1; it does not carry the humanized draft’s 73% survey statistic forward without original-source verification. Post 10 retains **Book an intro call** exactly.

Two structures use a discussion question; three use a practical application; four close without an interaction request; one uses the approved offer. This mix follows the ten reader jobs and is not a recommended platform ratio.

The existing artworks are not rebuilt here. Before a later copy/visual release, reconcile every changed caption with its panels, source labels, accessibility text and release manifest. The current update makes no claim of native LinkedIn preview, social publication or audience testing.

## 01-the-reason — Make the founder’s reasoning visible

**Pattern:** P13. **Reader job:** Understand what the proposed Outspoken service makes easier to explain.

**Question:** What does a product description leave unexplained for a prospective buyer?

**Point:** A proposed content brief should include the founder’s decisions and their relevance to a buyer question.

**Diagnosis:** The current drafts mix an observation, a service introduction and a broad final question. The reasoning behind the service can be made more concrete.

### Opening options

- A product description can tell a buyer what exists. The decision behind it can explain why it was built that way.
- What should a buyer understand about the choice behind your product?

**Payoff obligation:** Name one decision that belongs in a source interview, and show how it connects to a buyer’s evaluation.

### Functional sequence

1. **Recognisable gap:** Name the distinction between a feature description and the reason a team chose it. Present this as an editorial observation, not measured buyer behaviour.
2. **Concrete material:** Use an explicitly illustrative decision: which job to solve first, which alternative was rejected, or where human review stays.
3. **Reasoning and method:** Explain that a decision reveals a priority and trade-off. Describe the proposed interview → source check → original draft process.
4. **Bounded conclusion:** State that the immediate deliverable is a clearer account of the reasoning. Commercial outcomes require separate observation.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · source_confirmed:** Outspoken’s approved service positioning starts from founders and the business they are building. Source: [docs/APPROVED-POSITIONING.json](../../docs/APPROVED-POSITIONING.json). Boundary: The positioning is an aim, not delivered customer performance.
- **C02 · not_available_do_not_assert:** A particular founder made the example decision. Source: Founder interview or decision record required. Boundary: No actual founder story is supplied; retain the example as illustrative.

**Reasoning:** A documented choice exposes the priorities behind a product. Connecting those priorities to the reader’s decision explains relevance without asserting a conversion effect.

**Proposed close:** Start the content brief with the decision a buyer needs to understand.

**Audience interaction:** none.  The observation has a complete takeaway; a broad question would add little information.

**Humor:** optional. A feature list rarely tells you what the team argued about. original illustrative wording; use only if it fits the founder’s voice

**Format:** The existing carousel can assign one function to each panel, combining the bounded conclusion with the service context; do not imply that a fictional person is the founder.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm the author’s stance on decision-led content.
- Supply one publishable decision if converting this observation into P02.

## 02-what-we-actually-saw — A research number needs its denominator

**Pattern:** P01. **Reader job:** Understand what a selected LinkedIn reference sample can support.

**Question:** How should we use a median from a reference sample when choosing what to write?

**Point:** The preserved sample can inform questions and structural hypotheses; its reaction median is not an account target or a causal content rule.

**Diagnosis:** The evidence-led version already preserves denominators and limits. The migration makes the evidence → interpretation → usable decision sequence explicit.

### Opening options

- Our reference sample has a median of 107.5 reactions. What can we actually learn from it?
- There are 279 posts in our reference set. The useful next step is to explain what each observation can support.

**Payoff obligation:** Explain the median’s denominator, why the sample is not a benchmark, and which writing questions the research can help generate.

### Functional sequence

1. **Hook plus observation:** Introduce 107.5 as the median among the 276 posts with a reaction count; do not make it a target.
2. **Sample and comparison:** State the selected corpus of 279 posts and 61 accounts. If including comments, retain 36.5 across 256 posts as a separate denominator.
3. **Reasoning and limits:** Different authors, ages and topics limit comparisons. Reaction counts do not identify reach, clicks, buyer quality or sales.
4. **Practical application:** Use a post to investigate its reader question, evidence and explanation. Identify candidate structures for original work.
5. **Conclusion and source:** End with the research decision, followed by the historical capture window and a source link. No extra discussion question is necessary.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · recomputed:** The preserved sample contains 279 posts and 61 accounts. Source: [reports/20261105/data.json](../../reports/20261105/data.json). Boundary: Selected historical corpus; no claim of full LinkedIn coverage.
- **C02 · recomputed:** Median reactions are 107.5 among 276 observed counts; median comments are 36.5 among 256. Source: [reports/20261105/data.json](../../reports/20261105/data.json). Boundary: Different observed subsets; metrics in this report are timestamped 22 October 2026; the wider historical collection ran 20–22 October.
- **C03 · unsupported_do_not_assert:** A format caused higher reach or sales. Source: No identifying design or reach/conversion data. Boundary: Do not assert this claim or imply it from rankings.

**Reasoning:** A statistic answers a question about the observed rows. It cannot answer a different question about a new account’s distribution or revenue without additional evidence and design.

**Proposed close:** Use the sample to choose a question worth testing, with its evidence and limits attached.

**Audience interaction:** none.  The post closes the measurement question; the source link supplies the next useful action.

**Humor:** omit.  Keep the numerical distinction easy to read literally.

**Format:** If the existing five-panel layout is later rebuilt, separate sample, result, limitation and application. Keep denominator and capture date legible alongside the number.

**Required author inputs:**

- Approve the proposed interpretation in the agency voice.
- Confirm whether to include both metrics or retain only reactions for focus.

## 03-the-interview — Find the story inside a decision

**Pattern:** P04. **Reader job:** See how an interview can supply the material for an authentic professional story.

**Question:** What should an editor ask before drafting a founder’s story?

**Point:** Ask for the goal, constraint, considered alternatives, decision, observation and present interpretation before choosing a dramatic opening.

**Diagnosis:** Both drafts describe interview questions, but neither contains an actual interviewed founder story. A clearer process post avoids pretending that the missing interview already exists.

### Opening options

- Before writing a founder’s story, ask which decision the story needs to explain.
- A useful interview question: what made the other option reasonable at the time?

**Payoff obligation:** Show how to turn interview answers into a supported decision sequence, including rejected alternatives and uncertainty.

### Functional sequence

1. **Task and input:** Define the input as a founder’s actual decision, not a polished quotation.
2. **Decision prompts:** Ask what the goal was, what constrained the choice, and which alternatives were considered. Preserve what was known at the time.
3. **Consequence prompts:** Ask what happened afterwards, what evidence exists and what remains uncertain. Keep the author’s interpretation separate.
4. **Editorial bridge:** Map the answers to one buyer question. Explain which material becomes scene, choice, consequence and reflection.
5. **Review and application:** Have the founder confirm facts, voice and disclosure boundaries; invite the reader to try the interview prompt.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · proposal_for_review:** This is the proposed Outspoken interview workflow. Source: [docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md](../../docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md). Boundary: Not a report of an interview already delivered to a client.
- **C02 · not_available_do_not_assert:** An actual founder chose X because Y and then observed Z. Source: Permissioned interview and supporting record required. Boundary: No dialogue, motive, date or outcome may be invented.

**Reasoning:** Alternatives and constraints make a decision intelligible. Observed consequences and present reflection make a lesson assessable; neither requires a heroic success arc.

**Proposed close:** For the next interview, ask what made the rejected option plausible.

**Audience interaction:** application. Choose one decision and record the goal, alternatives and information available at the time. The reader can use the method without being asked to leave a generic comment.

**Humor:** optional. The interview is allowed to contain unfinished sentences. Original process observation, not a claim about a real interview.

**Format:** A carousel may show interview prompts and their narrative roles. Do not manufacture a quoted exchange to fill a panel.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm the proposed interview and review process.
- For a story version, supply one decision, alternatives, consequence and permission to use the details.

## 04-positioning — Test whether a claim explains a difference

**Pattern:** P05. **Reader job:** Diagnose a vague product statement before rewriting it.

**Question:** What information is missing when a product statement could apply to many companies?

**Point:** A useful positioning check asks who the buyer is, what job is involved, what differs and what supports the difference.

**Diagnosis:** The current drafts contain a useful generic phrase but move quickly from vagueness to the agency process. The method needs an inspectable diagnostic and bounded example.

### Opening options

- “AI-powered workflows” leaves four buyer questions unanswered.
- Could a buyer name the job your product changes from the first sentence?

**Payoff obligation:** Identify the missing buyer, task and substantiated difference; do not stop at criticising a generic phrase.

### Functional sequence

1. **Illustrative starting phrase:** Label “AI-powered workflows” as an invented generic example, not a quotation from a customer or competitor.
2. **Diagnostic criteria:** Ask: which buyer, which task, which alternative, and which supported difference? Use these as checks, not validated predictors.
3. **Worked rewrite:** Use a hypothetical request-review tool: it drafts a response from supplied material and routes it for human review. Do not add time savings or autonomy claims.
4. **Reasoning and conclusion:** Explain which uncertainty each detail removes. End with a bounded self-check rather than promising conversion gains.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · explicitly_illustrative:** The generic phrase and request-review example are illustrative. Source: Original editorial example. Boundary: No named product, real customer or competitor quotation.
- **C02 · unsupported_do_not_assert:** Clearer positioning causes a specific sales improvement. Source: No study or measured client result in this post. Boundary: Do not assert an uplift.

**Reasoning:** Naming the actor, task and difference gives the reader terms with which to compare the product. That explanatory benefit is the proposed purpose of the exercise.

**Proposed close:** Check whether a reader can name the task and the supported difference without borrowing your internal vocabulary.

**Audience interaction:** diagnostic_question. Which question does your current first sentence leave unanswered: buyer, task, alternative, or evidence? The answer adds specific diagnostic information; avoid a general “thoughts?” prompt.

**Humor:** optional. The phrase is broad enough to attend several companies’ all-hands meetings. Original exaggeration about wording; keep the real product explanation literal.

**Format:** A comparison graphic can show an invented vague sentence beside the same example with actor, task and review boundary labelled. Label it as illustrative.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm that the diagnostic fits the agency’s positioning process.
- Replace the hypothetical example only with an approved product account.

## 05-proof — Build a claim the reader can inspect

**Pattern:** P04. **Reader job:** Turn a broad AI claim into an evaluable explanation.

**Question:** What must a product claim show before a buyer can evaluate it?

**Point:** State the input, output, human intervention and operating conditions; attach a suitable source to each factual result.

**Diagnosis:** The humanized draft uses a survey statistic from a secondary report; the evidence-v1 draft already offers a cleaner hypothetical workflow. The structure update uses the latter and does not silently carry forward the survey.

### Opening options

- Before calling a workflow automated, show where a person still has to decide.
- A product claim becomes easier to evaluate when its inputs and limits are visible.

**Payoff obligation:** Give the reader a practical way to identify what the system does, what a person does and what the evidence actually demonstrates.

### Functional sequence

1. **Specific claim problem:** Introduce “automates request review” as a hypothetical claim requiring a defined scope.
2. **Visible demonstration:** Describe a proposed demonstration: approved input → generated suggestion → human review → final action. Mark it as illustrative.
3. **Evidence and warrant:** Explain that a working demonstration can establish an observed behaviour under stated conditions; reliability and time savings need repeated measurement with a denominator.
4. **Boundary and decision:** State the failure or review condition. Recommend replacing the broad claim with the behaviour the available evidence can support.
5. **Finished takeaway:** End with a precise claim-writing rule. Avoid adding an unrelated authority statistic.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · explicitly_illustrative:** The proposed request-review sequence is an example. Source: Original editorial example. Boundary: No product execution, benchmark or customer result was observed.
- **C02 · not_carried_forward:** Candidate carry-forward of the 73% survey statistic; applicability to this argument is unverified. Source: Original report and question would require separate verification. Boundary: Omitted from this structure; the old draft remains a historical variant.
- **C03 · not_available_do_not_assert:** A particular system reliably saves a stated amount of time. Source: Defined test, baseline, sample, period and measurement needed. Boundary: Do not infer reliability or savings from one screenshot.

**Reasoning:** The evidence burden rises from showing one behaviour to estimating its reliability, and again when attributing an outcome to the product. The wording should stay at the demonstrated level.

**Proposed close:** Write the claim at the level your evidence can support, and put its operating condition beside it.

**Audience interaction:** none.  The post gives a complete claim-writing rule; no audience answer is necessary.

**Humor:** omit.  The central task is to keep claim scope literal.

**Format:** A claim/evidence/boundary diagram can explain the logic. A fictional product screen must not appear to be captured customer evidence.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm the proposed claim-review workflow.
- Provide actual product demonstration material before turning the hypothetical into a case.

## 06-product-to-buyer — Explain the feature through a workflow

**Pattern:** P04. **Reader job:** Translate technical product language into an understandable user decision.

**Question:** What happens immediately before and after the feature is used?

**Point:** A workflow explanation should connect the trigger, input, operation, output, human responsibility and next decision.

**Diagnosis:** The current versions already ask useful workflow questions. The update adds the causal bridge between each step and the buyer’s understanding, without claiming that every buyer prefers this form.

### Opening options

- To explain an AI feature, start with the moment someone needs to act.
- Where does your product enter the workflow, and what happens after its output?

**Payoff obligation:** Provide a usable workflow map and the questions needed to fill it accurately.

### Functional sequence

1. **Reader situation:** Name the operational moment in which a person needs an answer or action.
2. **Workflow material:** Map trigger, input, operation, output, human review and next decision. Use one clearly hypothetical example unless real material is approved.
3. **Technical relevance:** Explain which model or architectural detail changes quality, latency, review or failure conditions. Keep those relevant details.
4. **Application and boundary:** Invite the reader to sketch one feature and mark unresolved questions. A sketch describes a process; it does not validate the implementation.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · proposal_for_review:** The workflow sequence is a proposed explanatory method. Source: [docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md](../../docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md). Boundary: Editorial usefulness is a reasoned hypothesis; no conversion outcome established.
- **C02 · not_available_do_not_assert:** The example describes the client’s actual implementation. Source: Technical owner and product documentation required. Boundary: Until verified, label the example hypothetical.

**Reasoning:** Connecting each input to an output and responsible person shows how the feature participates in a decision. Technical terms remain useful when they explain a material constraint.

**Proposed close:** Sketch the path from the trigger to the next human decision, then fill the first gap a new reader would encounter.

**Audience interaction:** application. Use one feature and mark the input, output, reviewer and next action. An actionable exercise is more relevant than requiring a comment.

**Humor:** optional. The model has a name. The person waiting for its output has a task. Original contrast for emphasis; optional and not evidence.

**Format:** A single diagram can carry the workflow while the caption explains a constraint. Keep source labels and human-review boundaries visible.

**Required author inputs:**

- Approve any real substituted workflow with its technical owner.
- Choose one detail the intended buyer actually needs to assess.

## 07-two-voices — Give founder and company publications different jobs

**Pattern:** P10. **Reader job:** Decide which parts of one subject belong in a founder post and a company explanation.

**Question:** How can two accounts address one buyer question without duplicating the same text?

**Point:** A proposed division is founder judgment and trade-off alongside company-owned product context or supporting material; it depends on the actual sources and voice.

**Diagnosis:** Both drafts avoid claiming founder-account superiority. The new structure makes the comparison criteria explicit and shows where the proposed division may not apply.

### Opening options

- One buyer question can need a founder’s judgment and a product team’s explanation.
- Before adapting a founder post for the company page, decide what new information the second post adds.

**Payoff obligation:** Show a division of information around the same buyer question, with a reason for assigning each part.

### Functional sequence

1. **Shared question:** Choose one illustrative buyer question, such as why a workflow retains human review.
2. **Comparison criteria:** Founder version: actual decision and trade-off. Company version: current workflow, supporting evidence and operating boundary. Mark this as a proposed allocation.
3. **Reasoning:** The two pieces add different information about the same subject; that is a coherence decision, not a ranking of account types.
4. **Exception:** A founder can present technical evidence and a company can explain decisions. Source ownership and audience context may call for another split.
5. **Conclusion:** Assign each piece to the voice able to explain and substantiate it; do not duplicate text solely to fill a calendar.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · proposal_for_review:** This division of content is an editorial proposal. Source: [docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md](../../docs/SERVICES-AND-WRITING.md). Boundary: No claim that one account type causes better reach or leads.
- **C02 · unsupported_do_not_assert:** Founder accounts outperform company accounts causally. Source: Historical corpus does not identify this effect. Boundary: Do not promote descriptive account medians to causal advice.

**Reasoning:** Comparison is useful when its criteria are visible. Here the criteria are information added, source authority and reader need, not aggregate reaction rankings.

**Proposed close:** Give the second publication a distinct piece of evidence or explanation to contribute.

**Audience interaction:** none.  The allocation rule closes the comparison; discussion can be added only if a concrete unresolved choice remains.

**Humor:** omit.  No joke needed to distinguish the two roles.

**Format:** Use a two-column information map with the shared buyer question above it. Do not title the columns winner and loser.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm the account roles and source ownership.
- Supply a real shared buyer question if moving beyond an illustrative comparison.

4. **Bounded conclusion:** The visual explains or illustrates; only appropriately sourced material can serve as evidence for a factual claim.

### Evidence and inference

**Proposed close:** Write the visual’s question first, then remove any element that competes with its answer.

**Audience interaction:** application. Name the one relationship your next image needs to show. A practical brief instruction fits the diagnostic purpose.

## 09-measure — Choose the measure from the question

**Pattern:** P05. **Reader job:** Decide what an available content metric can answer and what remains unknown.

**Question:** Which question can the observed measure actually answer?

**Point:** Start with the decision, match it to an observable measure, and retain missing reach or conversion data as unknown.

**Diagnosis:** The humanized version overlaps with post 02 by repeating medians; evidence-v1 already uses missingness counts. This structure gives both variants a clearer shared role: a measurement decision guide.

### Opening options

- Before choosing a LinkedIn metric, write the question you need it to answer.
- A visible reaction count answers one narrow question: how many reactions were observed?

**Payoff obligation:** Distinguish observed reactions, substantive questions, exposure and commercial outcomes without claiming that one metric substitutes for another.

### Functional sequence

1. **Decision:** Name the question first: response, exposure, useful discussion, inquiry or commercial outcome.
2. **Measurement map:** Reaction count describes recorded reactions; comments may contain questions; exposure needs actual impression or reach data; a commercial claim needs its own outcome definition and evidence.
3. **Worked limitation:** In the preserved sample, reactions exist for 276 of 279 posts and comments for 256. No reach or conversion evidence is available in that sample.
4. **Reasoning:** Different denominators and unavailable outcomes make some comparisons unanswerable. Do not turn missing data into zero or infer buyer intent from a reaction.
5. **Conclusion and invitation:** Choose the next observable question. Ask about a concrete gap only if the author wants that discussion.

### Evidence and inference

- **C01 · recomputed:** Observed reaction and comment counts cover 276 and 256 of the 279 posts. Source: [reports/20261105/data.json](../../reports/20261105/data.json). Boundary: Different observed subsets; metrics in this report are timestamped 22 October 2026.
- **C02 · unsupported_do_not_assert:** A reaction establishes buyer intent or a sale. Source: No buyer-intent or outcome record. Boundary: The inference is unsupported.
- **C03 · conditional:** A comment may contain a useful question. Source: Review of the actual comment would be required. Boundary: Do not claim a substantive question merely from the comment count.

**Reasoning:** A measure can be useful without answering every question. The next collection or review task should address the specific missing link between the available observation and the decision.

**Proposed close:** Choose the question, record the measure you have, and leave the unsupported answer open.

**Audience interaction:** bounded_question. Which decision are you trying to make that your current content metrics cannot answer? The response supplies a specific measurement problem, not a vote for agreement.

**Humor:** omit.  Keep the distinction between observation and outcome literal.

**Format:** A small question/measure/unknown table is sufficient. Do not use a conversion funnel diagram that implies observed conversions.

**Required author inputs:**

- Confirm which measurement questions the proposed service can actually support.
- Any client result needs separately authorized analytics and its own measurement definition.
