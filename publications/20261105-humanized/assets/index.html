02 / FROM OUR RESEARCH

279 posts changed the questions we ask.

Read the post
We built a reference set of 279 LinkedIn posts from 61 accounts. One result made the research more useful: the median reaction count was 107.5 across the 276 posts where a reaction count was available. That number is a starting point, not a target. The accounts, topics and post ages differ. We did not observe reach, clicks or sales, so the sample cannot tell us which format caused a business result. What it can do is sharpen the questions we ask: Is the post explaining a decision? Is the claim supported? Would the right buyer learn something useful? That is how we want to use research at Outspoken: identify patterns, check their limits and turn them into hypotheses to test with each client. 03 / THE INTERVIEW

The best sentence may be buried in a decision.

Read the post
A founder does not need to arrive with a polished LinkedIn post. I would rather start with a decision: What did you change? What else did you consider? Why did you choose this route? Those questions give an editor something a generic prompt cannot supply: the founder’s actual reasoning. From there, we can identify a buyer question, draft an angle and check which details can be shared. The founder reviews the copy before anything is ready to post. If a detail is confidential or a result cannot be verified, it stays out. A good interview should leave the founder with less pressure to perform and the reader with a clearer idea of how the company thinks.

04 / POSITIONING

If everyone can say it, buyers can't use it.

Read the post
“We use AI to transform workflows” leaves me with a question: whose workflow, and what changes? A positioning exercise should make that answer easier to give. At Outspoken, our approach starts with the customer’s task, the alternatives they already use and the difference the product can support with evidence. Then we test the language against a simple standard: could a buyer explain it back without using our internal vocabulary? That becomes the foundation for a founder’s profile and content—not just a line in a brand document. The aim is not to make a company sound bigger. It is to make the reason it matters easier to understand.

05 / EARN TRUST

A bold claim needs a boundary.

Read the post
“Our AI changes everything” is a hard claim for a buyer to evaluate. I would rather show the task it performs, what goes in, what comes out and where a person checks the result. If we quote a number, the period, method and denominator should travel with it. There is a reason to take that work seriously. In the 2024 Edelman–LinkedIn thought-leadership survey, 73% of decision-makers said an organisation’s thought leadership was a more trustworthy basis for assessing its capabilities than its marketing materials and product sheets. That is a survey response, not evidence that one LinkedIn post will win a deal. Our job is to help founders turn the evidence they can actually share into clear content, with the important limits intact. 06 / PRODUCT EDUCATION

A model name isn't a buyer's workflow.

Read the post
When a founder explains an AI feature, I want to know what happens just before—and just after—the model is used. What triggers the work? What information goes in? What does the product suggest? Who checks it, and what happens next? Those details are often more useful to a buyer than another model name. They reveal how the product fits a real process and where responsibility stays with a person. We help turn that explanation into a post, a diagram or a short script. The format follows the explanation; the founder checks the technical detail before release. Try this with one feature: draw its path from trigger to human decision. Where would a new buyer still have a question?

07 / FOUNDER + COMPANY

One story can have two useful voices.

Read the post
I do not think a founder profile and a company page need to repeat each other. The founder can explain the thinking: the decision, the trade-off and what changed their view. The company can make the supporting material easy to find: a product walkthrough, an approved example or a clear explanation of a limitation. Both should answer questions the same buyer actually has. Otherwise the founder sounds personal while the company sounds like a different business. That is how we would plan the two channels at Outspoken. We would also check the responses separately: our selected reference sample does not prove that a founder profile causes better results than a company page. Which part of your company’s story is easier for the founder to explain?

08 / THE VISUAL

A good graphic answers a question.

Read the post
Before designing a LinkedIn image, I want to know what the image makes easier to understand. A simple workflow can show where an AI system stops and human review begins. A comparison can show the trade-off between two approaches. A founder photograph can put a person behind a point of view. If a graphic cannot make its relationship clear without a long explanation, it probably needs another edit. Our process pairs the written argument with the visual brief, then keeps the founder’s review in the loop. We do not treat a polished image as evidence that the underlying claim is true. What is the one thing your next visual should help a buyer see?

09 / MEASUREMENT

A reaction is a signal. It isn't a sale.

Read the post
In our selected sample of 279 LinkedIn posts, the median was 107.5 reactions among the 276 posts with an observed reaction count. The median was 36.5 comments among the 256 with an observed comment count. A reaction combines LinkedIn reaction types. It is not a comment, an impression, a qualified buyer or a sale. The two medians also use different sets of posts. I find those distinctions useful because they change what we do next. A comment may contain a question worth answering. A reaction tells us only that someone reacted. A real buyer conversation gives us a different kind of evidence. We use the numbers to decide what to investigate, with similar posts and observation windows where possible. The sample was captured 20–22 October 2026 and is not a platform benchmark. We did not observe reach or conversions. Research and limitations: https://github.com/mcharniuk1-spec/linkedin/tree/main/reports/20261105
