If your product statement could describe twenty companies, inspect what it leaves out.

Start with four questions.

Who is the buyer, and in what situation are they deciding? What task are they trying to complete? What would they do instead? What supported difference would change that choice?

A hypothetical example: "AI for better operations" leaves the work undefined. "Flag requests for a human reviewer against a stated rule" names an action and a review boundary. It still needs a buyer, a real alternative and evidence before it becomes a complete product claim.

The point is to make the decision assessable. A clearer sentence does not, by itself, prove a sales improvement.

Which question does your first sentence leave unanswered: buyer, task, alternative or evidence?
