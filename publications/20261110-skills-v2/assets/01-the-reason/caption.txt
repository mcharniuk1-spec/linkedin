A feature list tells a buyer what exists. A decision can explain why it exists that way.

Which problem did the team choose to solve first? What did it rule out? Where did it keep a person in the loop?

Those details reveal priorities and trade-offs. They give a reader something more useful to assess than a list of capabilities.

That is the starting point of the content workflow we are proposing at Outspoken: recover a real decision, connect it to a buyer question, check the facts, then write an original account of the reasoning.

The immediate aim is clearer communication. Whether that earns attention, qualified conversations or sales is something to observe separately.

Start the brief with the decision a buyer needs to understand.
