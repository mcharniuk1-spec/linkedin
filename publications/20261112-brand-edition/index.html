01 · Founder / business owner

Someone read your post. What happens next?

A post gets someone interested. Then what? They visit the profile. They look for an example. They ask a question. Each step needs to give them a useful reason to continue. That is the path I want OutSpoken to build: • Understand the business, its buyers and the founder’s point of view. • Make the profile explain who the work is for and what it changes. • Turn real questions and decisions into useful posts and visual examples. • Review the facts, publish the agreed material and respond with context. • When outreach is in scope, research suitable companies and carry the actual conversation into the next step. A shared document holds the source material. A content board connects each post to a buyer question. A CRM or simple Sheet keeps the reply and agreed follow-up visible. For example, a team selling a reporting tool could show how someone checks an answer against its source. The profile points to that example. A reply can then start with the reader’s own reporting problem. Voice builds the profile and content foundation. Voice + Pipeline adds the research, outreach and qualification around it. Start by checking one handoff in your own path. After someone reads your post, can they find the next useful thing?

02 · Founder / marketer

A content pillar is not a topic label

“AI”, “leadership” and “our journey” can fill a content calendar. They still leave the writer with a basic question: what should the reader understand? I would build a pillar around a buyer decision. For an illustrative invoice-approval product, that gives us three different jobs: • Is the problem worth solving? Show where an invoice gets delayed. • Is this approach suitable? Compare manual review with rule-based routing. • Can our team use it? Walk through one invoice, including an exception. Now the topic has a purpose. The founder can explain the choice. The designer has something specific to show. The buyer gets a way to assess the approach. Put four fields in the content board: buyer question, source material, useful example and next action. If an idea cannot fill those fields, develop it before asking someone to write it. That is how a content pillar becomes a working brief.

03 · Founder / non-technical team

One founder conversation can do more than produce quotes

The useful part of a founder interview is often the detail that sounds ordinary to the founder. Why did you ask for a sample before connecting live data? Why did you keep a manual check? What did the alternative make harder? Those details contain the reasoning a buyer needs. In a shared document, capture four things: • The decision and the alternative. • The buyer’s question. • An example we can show or explain. • The position the founder can stand behind. Then use comments to resolve missing facts. Keep the founder’s actual wording where it carries meaning. If nobody knows the consequence yet, leave that question open. One conversation may support several posts when each answers a different buyer question. The cadence follows the material and the time available to review it. Bring one product or service decision to the next interview. Ask for the reason behind it.

An interview becomes useful when it reaches a choice, an alternative and something the reader can inspect. These are the prompts we would start with.

04 · Founder / consultant

Your profile has to finish the job your post started

Your post makes a useful point. A buyer opens your profile and finds “Founder | AI | Innovation”. They still have to work out what you do for someone like them. Check the profile as the continuation of the post: • Headline: who do you help, and with what work? • About: what problem do you understand, and how do you approach it? • Featured: which example helps the visitor assess that explanation? • Recent posts: what can they learn about your judgment? For OutSpoken, a headline to test could be: “Building OutSpoken | LinkedIn content and buyer conversations for B2B teams.” The rest of the profile would need to support it with the actual service and useful work to inspect. Use a document with the current wording, proposed wording and reason for each change. A stronger adjective is less useful than removing one thing the buyer has to guess. Open your Featured section. Does it help a visitor take the next step your posts invite?

05 · Founder / small team

Choose the jobs before choosing the tool stack

Before adding another tool, name the handoff it should improve. A founder-led LinkedIn programme needs a few clear jobs: • Capture product facts and useful examples in a shared document. • Plan topics in a board with the buyer question, owner and review date. • Review claims and voice with visible edits. • Preview and publish through the agreed account workflow. • Research suitable companies when outreach is part of the scope. • Keep actual replies and next actions in a CRM or Sheet. Google Docs, native LinkedIn, a simple board and a Sheet can cover much of the starting workflow. A specialist tool becomes useful when it solves a specific constraint. The client’s side matters too. Who supplies the material? Who checks facts? Who owns the account or subscription? At OutSpoken, I want each tool to have a job, an owner and a cost we can explain. Start with the slowest handoff, then choose what helps it.

06 · Founder / sales

Give someone a reason to reply

A first message needs a reason to exist. “Thanks for connecting. We help businesses grow” asks the recipient to supply that reason themselves. Start with something you checked and a useful next step. For example, if a company has publicly announced a partner programme, a message could be: “I saw you’re building the partner programme. Would a short checklist for turning recurring onboarding questions into founder posts be useful?” The observation needs to be real. The resource needs to exist. A possible problem should stay a possibility until the person confirms it. Keep three fields beside the draft: checked context, relevance to the person’s work and the small next step. Use a CRM or Sheet to record the actual answer. Voice + Pipeline connects this research with the same buyer understanding used in the content. A large contact list cannot replace a clear reason for writing to one person.

07 · Founder / sales

What do you send when a prospect says “send me details”?

“Send me details” gives you something concrete to answer. I would send the relevant detail first, then ask the question that helps the person use it. A reply could be: “Of course. Here’s a short example of how we turn founder material into a post. It shows the source, draft and review steps. Which part is taking the most time for you: finding the material, writing, or getting it approved?” That answer gives them something to inspect. Their response changes the recommendation. An ideas problem may need a better content brief. A voice problem needs better source material and editing. Slow review needs a clearer decision and owner. Record the requested resource, actual reply and agreed next action in the contact record. Keep follow-up within the conversation and contact rules you have agreed. The next reply should make the situation clearer for both people.

08 · Founder / marketer

One strong piece of work can open several conversations

One useful source can support several posts. The buyer question is what makes them different. Take a reviewed product workflow. The founder can explain why the team chose that approach. A practical post can show how one task works, including an exception. A checklist can help a buyer compare it with their current process. Those are three different uses of the same knowledge. Put the source in a shared document. In the content board, give each proposed post its own reader, question, example and takeaway. Keep the factual limits attached to every version. Simply cutting one paragraph into three smaller posts adds little. Changing the decision you help the reader make gives the material a new use. Choose one substantial piece of work you already have. Look for an explanation, a demonstration and a way to help someone choose.

09 · Founder / business owner

Your champion is not the whole buying group

Your product champion may understand the feature. Other people in the buying group still have their own questions. The user wants to know how the task changes. The manager needs to understand adoption. Finance wants a credible account of cost and value. Security or legal may ask who controls the data and checks the result. These questions belong in the content plan. For an illustrative reporting tool, one post could show a worked answer, another could explain setup, and another could show when a person checks the output. Make a small buyer-question grid in the strategy document. Add the role, decision, useful evidence and next question. Use the client’s actual buying process to fill it. A post will not complete a purchasing process. It can give the champion a clearer explanation to share with the rest of the team. Which role does your current explanation leave doing the most guessing?

13 · Manager / non-technical team

Give the reviewer a decision to make

“Any feedback?” leaves several different decisions inside one vague request. One person changes the tone. Another questions a fact. Someone rewrites the call to action. The draft keeps moving, but the reason it is waiting stays unclear. Put three checks beside it: • Product truth: does this match what we deliver? • Founder position: is this the view and wording the author can stand behind? • Reader action: does the ending give a useful next step? Name the reviewer for each decision. Use document suggestions for wording changes and comments for the reason. A useful review note is specific: “This sentence promises a delivery time we have not agreed. Confirm the commitment or remove the time claim.” That gives the writer a decision they can act on. It also lets non-technical colleagues help without guessing at the product facts.

16 · Founder / content lead

How often should a founder post?

How often should a founder post? Start with the material and review time available, then choose a rhythm the team can support. A founder may have one substantial decision to explain. A practitioner may have several teachable examples. A company Page may need to clarify product changes for different buying roles. Put five things in the plan: reader purpose, usable source material, reviewer capacity, useful format and a review point. A busy founder could test one well-supported post a week. A team with enough distinct examples could test two or three. These are planning options, not a formula for performance. At OutSpoken, we agree the starting cadence during the 45-minute call. Then we review the workload, the questions buyers ask and whether the material stays useful. Choose a starting rhythm. Give it a review date. Change it because of what you learn.
