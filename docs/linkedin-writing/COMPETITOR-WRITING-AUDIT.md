## What to adopt

Use a source interview to recover a real choice, constraint, alternative and consequence. Preserve the author’s vocabulary and stance. Turn that material into a buyer-specific question before choosing a hook. Separate a story, a diagnostic, a practical method, a research observation and an offer; each needs different evidence. Require explicit author review and keep the claim ledger separate from prose. Reuse a supported insight across formats by changing the explanation for the reader, while retaining its source and limits.

For Outspoken, these are candidate operating choices, not a differentiated market claim. Interview-led writing, founder voice, buyer relevance and author review appear in several provider descriptions. A defensible distinction would require showing the actual source-to-claim reasoning and delivering useful, author-approved examples. Do not claim uniqueness, superior outcomes or a proven methodology from this comparison.

## What to leave as untested

Named content ratios, validated-hook language, broad authority-to-pipeline claims, algorithm assertions and before/after case totals remain provider claims or historical recorded methods. The older CRIT, 3Ps and radio-model references can inform questions or editorial categories. They do not establish a mandatory post sequence, posting quota, distribution effect or adoption right for proprietary materials. Original prose and reusable functional skeletons are appropriate; copied competitor wording and media are excluded.
