## Route A: argument or explanation

Use when the reader needs to understand, evaluate or act on a claim. Adapt the order to the selected pattern.

| Function | Content to supply | Claim/source IDs and check |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | The concrete situation, question or decision. | What establishes that context? |
| Bounded point | The answer, distinction, recommendation or provisional position. | Which claim type is it? |
| Evidence | The relevant observation, source, comparison or clearly labelled worked example. | What was actually accessed? What does it support? |
| Warrant | Explain why that material supports this point or changes the decision. | Is there an inference gap? Is a causal claim being implied? |
| Boundary | The condition, alternative explanation, exception or missing evidence that changes the conclusion. | What remains unknown or does not transfer? |
| Conclusion | The practical implication in proportion to the evidence. | Does it answer the opening without adding a stronger claim? |
| Optional response | One useful next action or a genuine, answerable question. | Does it serve the reader's job? Is any offer current and approved? |

Repeat an evidence–warrant unit only when the argument needs it. A citation beside a sentence does not explain the reasoning for the reader.

## Route B: confirmed founder decision

Use for P02, or adapt for the actual changed belief in P03. Complete the source confirmation before drafting a scene as fact.

**Author confirmation**

- Confirming author and date: [ ]
- Interview, note or record locator: [ ]
- Details confirmed by the author: [ ]
- Numerical outcomes supported by a separate record: [ ]
- Details unavailable or withheld: [ ]
- Permission for shared people, customer and product details: [ ]

| Function | Confirmed material | What the reader should understand |
|---|---|---|
| Moment | What was happening, when, and why it mattered. | The professional situation and stakes. |
| Goal and constraint | What the founder was trying to achieve with the information available then. | Why the choice was difficult. |
| Alternatives | The options actually considered. | What could reasonably have happened instead. |
| Decision and reason | What was chosen and why, at that time. | The founder's judgment, without hindsight certainty. |
| Consequence | What was observed, including mixed, unfinished or disappointing results. | Outcome distinguished from attribution. |
| Reflection | The author's present interpretation and any revised practice. | What changed in the author's understanding. |
| Transfer and limit | A usable lesson, question or condition. | Where the experience may and may not apply. |

Do not invent dialogue, a customer, a private feeling, an earlier belief or a successful recovery. If a recollection cannot be confirmed, leave it on hold and choose a suitable lower-burden pattern. A service proposal is not an event that already happened.
