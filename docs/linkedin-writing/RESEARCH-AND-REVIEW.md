## Charter

**Research question:** How should a professional LinkedIn publication connect an opening, evidence or experience, reasoning, paragraph sequence and an earned ending while retaining the author's voice?

The intended output is a reusable composition method for founder-led writing. The agency serves Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders with 10–50 people, Seed–Series B. A client's publication requires that client's own reader, product and evidence brief. The agency's audience is not a substitute for it.

This review combines official platform documentation, bounded scholarly research, preserved project assessments, current draft diagnostics and three identified agency pages. It examines hooks, proof, professional stories, discussion, humor and format selection. It does not establish a posting schedule, predict distribution or validate a commercial acquisition system. The collection security-verification hold remains unchanged. No publication, outreach or collection-runtime change follows from this methodology.

## Findings ledger

### LWR-F01 — Authenticity requires accountable authorship

LinkedIn's policies address misleading content, identity, spam and professional conduct. Its AI guidance permits assistance while expecting the member's perspective and review; disclosure of substantial AI assistance is recommended where it would not be obvious. This supports confirmed experience and author approval, not fabricated first-person scenes. A stylistic suspicion or analytics tip is not, by itself, a policy violation. **P/G:** L2-O01, L2-O02.

### LWR-F02 — Feed relevance is contextual; public descriptions are incomplete

LinkedIn describes semantic relevance, professional interests, interaction history and sequential ranking. Popularity inputs remain present in its engineering account. Its anti-slop publications describe classifiers and report effectiveness figures, but do not provide an independently audited prediction for an individual post. The defensible writing response is relevant substance, not a promised ranking technique. **D/M:** L2-O03–06.

### LWR-F03 — A useful opening names an intelligible question

LinkedIn's exposed creator guidance recommends an engaging opening. Curiosity theory concerns a perceived knowledge gap; laboratory curiosity studies do not validate a LinkedIn hook formula. A registered report using 8,977 Upworthy tests found that the concreteness–selection relationship varied with competing headlines' concreteness. Concreteness itself was measured rather than separately randomized. Draft a specific promise and fulfill it; neither maximum vagueness nor complete disclosure is universally optimal. **G/T/E:** L2-O07, L2-O08, LWT01–03.

### LWR-F04 — Specificity improves inspectability, not necessarily truth

LinkedIn's article guidance favors focused expertise and examples. Research abstracts suggest that concrete language can affect perceived attentiveness or truth, while abstract recommendations can also influence attitudes. These findings concern different settings and outcomes. Use concrete details to clarify a situation, then justify the general conclusion. Vividness, precision and confidence do not authenticate a claim. **G/T/E:** L2-O09, LWT04–06.

### LWR-F05 — A story can persuade without proving its general lesson

Narrative-transportation research links immersion with story-consistent beliefs and character evaluations; its experimental contexts are limited. It does not establish that stories outperform data on LinkedIn. A professional story should distinguish the situation, available information, alternatives, choice, observed consequence and later interpretation. Its transfer to another business needs a separate warrant. **T/E:** LWT07.

### LWR-F06 — Humor has a credibility trade-off

Humor research reports possible status benefits from appropriate successful humor and risks from inappropriate humor. Failed humor was not significantly harmful in every comparison. Research on humorous negative self-disclosure found possible warmth/competence benefits alongside reduced perceived truthfulness of nearby negative statements. Therefore, keep factual claims literal and jokes intelligible; amusement is neither trust nor product evidence. **T/E:** LWT08, LWT09.

### LWR-F07 — Formats provide different explanatory affordances

Posts, articles, newsletters and documents have different tools and constraints. Posts currently permit 3,000 characters; articles support longer structured treatment; newsletters create a recurring series with subscription behavior; documents support sequential visual explanation. The document guidance also covers downloadability and replacement limitations. Article-publishing rules have article-specific scope. None of these features proves a reach advantage. **D/P/E:** L2-O10, L2-O12–14, L2-O17.

### LWR-F08 — Availability, attention and business value are distinct

Public visibility can make content accessible outside LinkedIn; it does not guarantee impressions. LinkedIn defines approximate impressions, distinct members reached and several engagement, profile and link measures, with privacy and counting qualifications. Reactions, views or a comment count do not establish comprehension, trust, buyer fit, causality or sales. Evaluate a post against its reader job and available outcome evidence. **D/E:** L2-O11, L2-O15.

### LWR-F09 — Distribution prescriptions remain hypotheses

The reviewed official sources do not establish a universal external-link penalty, best format, mandatory question ending or optimal posting frequency. LinkedIn's twice-weekly/profile-view promotional claim lacks a disclosed causal design. A partly accessible insights hub and a best-practices pointer cannot fill that gap. Historical [R12](../../design%20and%20website/linkedin-analysis/TOPICS-AND-PUBLICATIONS-R12.md)/[R15](../../design%20and%20website/linkedin-analysis/PUBLICATIONS-R15.md) recommendations about format superiority, founder-profile superiority, offer avoidance and frequency are superseded as prescriptions; retain them as hypotheses with their descriptive observations. **G/M/E:** L2-O08, L2-O19, L2-O20; current architecture §10.

### LWR-F10 — Discussion needs a reason to exist

LinkedIn says it is reducing engagement bait and prohibits engagement pods and automated comments. This does not require every post to end with a question. The editorial conclusion is to ask for experience, a missing comparison or a counterexample only when the answer would advance the subject. A complete conclusion, useful application or relevant offer can stand alone. **D/P/E:** L2-O03, L2-O18.

### LWR-F11 — Research access and writing assistance are separate

LinkedIn restricts unauthorized scraping and automation. The project additionally preserves an explicit collection hold, disabled post-read access without approved scopes and existing source receipts. Official documentation research does not authorize reacquiring held creator resources through a different backend. The two excluded retrievals supplied no findings, quotations or patterns here. **P/L:** L2-O16, L2-O18, L2-R04.

### LWR-F12 — The corpus supports bounded pattern discovery

The preserved dataset records 279 posts, 61 accounts and 477 snapshots. Those accounts are professional references, not a list of competitors. The eight selected examples below are previous interpretations; their full copied post bodies are absent from this checkout. All eight have recorded performance status `insufficient_comparable_sample`. They support hypotheses about useful structures, not new close readings or a winning-post ranking. **C/L:** L2-C01–08, L2-R01.

The [complete field audit](corpus-audit/README.md) finds no join errors, but all 279 records retain unverified text completeness; messenger role, experience and message origin are uncoded. Seven historical source IDs remain unresolved. The November 5 report is an export of older evidence: its current metric observations fall on October 22, 08:39:11–14:42:10 UTC; separate capture receipts have their own dates. Consistent joins do not certify source truth.

### LWR-F13 — Repeated draft shapes are a review signal

Seven of ten evidence-v1 captions end with a question; four of ten humanized captions do. Eight of ten humanized captions contain five paragraphs, counted by blank-line separation. These are mechanical properties of drafts, not published performance results. They justify checking whether the ending and paragraph count follow the reader job rather than a repeated template. **L/E:** L2-R02, L2-R03.

### LWR-F14 — Provider processes support interview-and-review architecture

Windmill describes founder conversations about decisions and lessons, voice-based drafting and review. Hat Tip's Ammo account describes content buckets, biweekly interviews, drafting and weekly client review. These are first-party process descriptions. Hat Tip's outcome figures are promotional self-reports with concurrent distribution activity, not causal evidence for the writing method. Transfer the interview and approval design; do not import outreach or account-operation practices. **B/M/E:** L2-B01, L2-B02.

### LWR-F15 — Commercial comparison coverage is incomplete

SalesBread's page primarily describes personalized LinkedIn/email outbound. Reader-specific research is relevant to a writing brief; its lead-generation claims do not establish organic-content effectiveness. Only these three agencies are identified from the separate 15-agency study context; the remaining twelve are unresolved in this review. The 61 reference accounts are a different population. **B/E:** L2-B03; project coverage boundary.

### LWR-F16 — Architecture is a reviewable synthesis

The resulting method separates reader job, topic, pattern, medium and voice. Its argument unit connects a claim to relevant material, explains the link and states a consequence or limit. Its story unit makes a decision understandable without inventing missing experience. These are editorial design choices grounded in the preceding findings, not validated templates for reach or sales. **E:** LWR-F01–15.

## Scope filter

Apply these filters before promoting a source statement into a writing rule:

1. **Date and product scope:** distinguish current Help documentation from older guidance; do not convert relative update labels into invented dates. Article rules need not describe every post format.
2. **Population and outcome:** Upworthy clicks, laboratory judgments, customer-service satisfaction and LinkedIn buyer response are different objects. Explain any proposed transfer.
3. **Access and independence:** an abstract, selected pages, a provider's account and a prior machine-assisted interpretation warrant different claims. A second agent agreeing is not independent evidence.
4. **Missingness and selection:** a purposive corpus, incomplete competitor coverage and unavailable originals cannot support prevalence or an exhaustive best-practice ranking.
5. **Authority:** methodological review does not change the collector hold, authorize publishing or establish permission to reuse another author's text or media.

## Object filter

| Object | Question to answer | Keep separate from |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Whose decision or understanding should change? | The agency's own ICP when drafting for a client. |
| Topic | What is the publication about? | Its purpose or proof quality. |
| Editorial job and pattern | Does it explain, diagnose, narrate, compare, discuss or offer? | A fixed caption length or paragraph count. |
| Medium and media role | Does the asset demonstrate, explain, illustrate or provide atmosphere? | Evidence of a numerical or customer-outcome claim. |
| Claim | Is it observed, attributed, inferred, recommended or forecast? | Persuasive wording and author confidence. |
| Evaluation | What was measured, for whom and when? | Reference fit, audience interest, buying intent and causal impact. |

## Conclusions

**Build the evidence packet before choosing the opening.** LWR-F01, F03–05 and F16 support an opening whose promise matches available material. Missing personal experience should route to a method, observation or working question rather than an invented founder story.

**Use functions rather than fixed paragraph counts.** LWR-F07, F12 and F13 support selecting an appropriate pattern, then labeling each paragraph's contribution. Evidence needs explanation; a conclusion needs a visible connection to what precedes it.

**Choose the ending from the job.** LWR-F08–10 support a bounded takeaway, conditional decision rule, useful application, genuine question or relevant invitation. A question is optional; engagement is an incomplete outcome.

**Use humor without blurring the literal claim.** LWR-F04 and F06 support concrete recognition and careful separation of a joke from evidence. No humor technique here carries a credibility or distribution guarantee.

**Adopt workflow ideas with explicit limits.** LWR-F11 and F14–15 support founder interviews, source checks and author review while preserving access restrictions and incomplete competitor coverage.

## Next steps and risks

Use the architecture and pattern library on the current concepts, recording missing founder material and claim evidence. Review structure, facts, voice and format separately; a strong style score cannot compensate for fabrication or an unsupported causal promise. Obtain author review before treating a skeleton as publishable copy.

A future authorized four-to-six-week observational pilot may help discover useful audience questions and workflow problems. That duration is a planning heuristic, not an A/B design, a minimum statistically sufficient sample or proof of a distribution effect. Define the reader job, available measures and confounders first. Preserve negative and ambiguous observations alongside successes.

Remaining risks are changing platform documentation, partial scholarly access, absent original post bodies, selected examples, self-reported provider outcomes and twelve unresolved agencies. Refresh only the relevant evidence when authorized; do not close these gaps by relabeling interpretations as observations.
