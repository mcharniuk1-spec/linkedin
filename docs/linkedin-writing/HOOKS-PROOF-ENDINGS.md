# Hooks, proof and endings

Version 2.0 · 10 November 2026 · original editorial guidance for founder review.

Use this guide with the [writing architecture](../LINKEDIN-WRITING-ARCHITECTURE.md), [publication patterns](patterns.json), [humor guide](HUMOR-AND-INTEREST.md) and [source registry](source-registry.json). Pattern IDs below refer to P01–P14. Research IDs refer to LWT01–LWT09.

The practical rules are editorial synthesis, not validated recipes for LinkedIn reach. **Every sample sentence, dialogue and numerical case in this document is an original fictional illustration. First-person wording does not describe an actual founder's memory or a customer result.** Replace it with confirmed material before adapting it for publication. Apply the client's reader and voice brief; the agency's audience does not automatically become the client's audience.

## 1. Write the promise before polishing the opening

A useful working brief has four sentences: the reader is deciding something; the post offers a particular answer; specified material supports that answer; one meaningful limit remains. If those sentences are missing, a striking opening can conceal an unfinished argument.

The hook is a promise about the reading experience. It might promise an explanation, a decision criterion, an inspectable process or recognition of a work situation. The body must deliver that particular value. A result requires measurement context. A scene requires a consequential choice. A disagreement requires a fair account of the position being challenged.

Write the supported point first, then try two or three openings with different functions. Select for relevance, truthful specificity and a payoff already present in the draft. A quieter opening can fit a stronger argument. No fixed opening length or line count follows from this method.

## 2. Choose a hook family and keep its contract

These fictional examples demonstrate functions, not reusable claims about the author.

| Family and patterns | Illustrative opening | Payoff owed by the body |
|---|---|---|
| Observation with an unresolved explanation — P01, P13 | “The demo got shorter. The buying decision did not.” | Define what was observed, the comparison and what might explain the difference. |
| Decision under a constraint — P02, P10 | “We could ship the integration sooner if we accepted a weekly manual repair.” | Show real alternatives, the relevant constraint, the chosen trade-off and its cost. |
| Changed working rule — P03 | “I stopped treating completed onboarding screens as evidence of activation.” | Establish the earlier assumption, the disconfirming evidence and the revised criterion. |
| Precise diagnostic question — P05, P11 | “Does activation mean the first login or the first useful output?” | Explain what changes under each definition and what evidence would settle the choice. |
| Qualified counterposition — P07 | “Automating this handoff makes sense when exceptions are rare. Ours were daily.” | Represent the usual advice fairly, substantiate the exception and offer a narrower rule. |
| Inspectable process or change — P04, P09 | “Here is where a support request becomes a product decision.” | Show the input, transition, output and human judgment needed along the way. |
| Research translated into a decision — P06 | “This study measured headline clicks. Our decision concerns reader understanding.” | Report what was studied, its result and the limits of transferring it to the new question. |
| Bounded offer — P12 | “For teams reviewing their onboarding, this session maps the first useful customer outcome.” | Specify the actual offer, fit, scope, available support and approved next step. |

P08, a measured case, can lead with its observed result when the denominator, period and comparison can follow. P14, professional humor, can promise recognition and end with a payoff; it need not pretend to be a research argument.

**Weak:** “This changed everything about our growth.”

**Improved:** “We changed the activation definition before changing the onboarding flow.”

The second opening identifies a decision the body can explain. It still needs a real reason and record; specificity alone does not verify the claim.

**Weak:** “Nobody tells founders this.”

**Improved:** “A shorter trial gives you less time to observe a weekly workflow.”

The improved version exposes a concrete consideration. Its conclusion should depend on the actual workflow rather than asserting that every short trial is a mistake.

**Weak:** “The secret to product adoption? Keep reading.”

**Improved:** “Our next adoption check separates first use from repeated use.”

The improved version can reveal its point immediately. The remaining interest comes from the method and its consequences.

### What curiosity research supports

[LWT01: Loewenstein, 1994](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf) proposes an information-gap account: curiosity concerns an acknowledged difference between current and desired knowledge. It is a theory, not a prescribed amount of withholding. Selected relevant pages of the full scan were inspected.

[LWT03: Aubin Le Quéré and Matias, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11704130/) analysed 8,977 Upworthy headline tests. The association between concreteness and click-through changed with the concreteness of the alternatives. Whole headline variants were randomly displayed; the measured concreteness trait was not independently randomised. This does not establish an optimal LinkedIn hook, and clicks do not establish understanding. The practical inference is modest: inspect both excessive vagueness and an opening that exhausts its question without offering further value.

## 3. Match the claim to adequate support

“Proof” here means support appropriate to a claim's scope. One screenshot, recollection or percentage cannot answer every kind of question.

| Claim type | Support to collect | Wording boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive result | Source, numerator, denominator, unit, period, population and comparison basis. | State what changed in the observed case. |
| Causal explanation | A credible identification design, its assumptions and relevant competing explanations. | A before-and-after sequence alone supports “after” or “coincided with,” not automatic attribution. |
| Product capability | Actual demonstration or documentation, version, input, conditions, human intervention and failure limits. | One successful demonstration does not establish reliability across all inputs. |
| Personal recollection | Founder confirmation, context and permission; documents for numerical outcomes where available. | Attribute remembered impressions; do not invent dialogue or another person's private thoughts. |
| Recommendation | Defined goal, decision criteria, relevant evidence, alternatives and trade-offs. | Explain for whom and under which conditions the recommendation holds. |
| Forecast | Assumptions, horizon, model or reasoning, uncertainty and update conditions. | Preserve the difference between a projection, target and promise. |
| External finding | Source actually accessed, design, population, result and access limits. | An abstract supports a narrower account than inspected methods and results. |
| Comparison | Same relevant criteria, sufficiently comparable conditions and dated versions or costs. | A preferred option for one task is not universally superior. |

An attributed customer statement establishes what that person reported. A synthetic example illustrates a relationship. A proposed checklist organises decisions. Each can be useful within its role; none should silently become a measured customer outcome.

If the required evidence is unavailable, reduce the claim or change the pattern. P04 can explain a proposed workflow without claiming P08 customer savings. P11 can ask a genuine unresolved question without manufacturing a confident answer.

## 4. Make the reasoning visible

The warrant is the explanation connecting the material to the claim. Ask: **why would this observation change the reader's decision?** A table can establish a difference while leaving its importance unexplained. A story can show what happened while leaving the proposed lesson unsupported.

A useful paragraph has one dominant contribution. For an argument, the local movement can be claim → material → explanation → consequence or limit. These functions need not occupy four sentences. For a story, a paragraph can move from a constraint to a choice; the reflection can follow later.

**Weak fictional draft:** “The new onboarding worked. Activation increased 37.5%. This proves simple flows win.”

**Improved fictional draft:** “Activation rose from 80 of 1,000 sign-ups to 110 of 1,000: from 8% to 11%, a gain of 3 percentage points, or 37.5% relative to the initial rate. The onboarding flow changed during the comparison, but so did the campaign audience. The result gives us a reason to investigate the flow; it does not isolate its effect.”

The arithmetic describes the observed change. The audience change offers a competing explanation. A justified next step is to compare sufficiently similar cohorts or design an appropriate test. The numbers alone do not prove the mechanism, statistical reliability or future performance.

Useful transitions name the logical action: “The relevant comparison is…”, “This matters because…”, “Another explanation is…”, “This would change if…”. The words are optional; the connection is not. “Therefore” does not repair an unsupported inference.

Keep related sentences together. Break the paragraph when its contribution changes, when a scene moves or when a limit deserves clear attention. Repeated isolated lines do not necessarily create a sequence of ideas.

## 5. Give the boundary consequences

A meaningful limit changes interpretation or action. “Results may vary” rarely tells the reader how. Identify the relevant difference: team size, task frequency, acquisition source, product version, manual effort, measurement window or an untested failure condition.

For P08, ask what changed alongside the intervention. For P06, compare the study's population, task and measured outcome with the proposed application. For P02 and P03, separate what the founder knew at the time from the interpretation formed afterward. For P10, ask whether unequal baselines explain the preferred result.

Choose the strongest plausible alternative explanation relevant to the claim. The post need not list every imaginable caveat. If the alternative would overturn the headline, revise the headline; do not bury the issue after a sales invitation.

## 6. End at the level the evidence earns

The conclusion pays off the opening and leaves a useful next understanding or action. It should not introduce a larger claim than the body supports.

| Ending | When it fits | Fictional illustration |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded conclusion | The explanatory question has been answered. | “For this workflow, waiting between steps remains the part to investigate.” |
| Conditional rule | The post clarifies a decision criterion. | “When exceptions require daily repair, map them before estimating automation savings.” |
| Concrete application | The reader can perform a useful check. | “Separate processing time from waiting time in your next workflow review.” |
| Open uncertainty | The material leaves a consequential question unresolved. | “We still need to test whether the same handoff works with larger accounts.” |
| Focused interaction | Other experience could change or extend the answer. | “Which exception made you keep a human review step?” |
| Direct offer | P12 has established fit, scope and the actual service. | Use the approved next action associated with that offer. |

**Weak:** “That is why every founder must automate now.”

**Improved:** “This example identifies a handoff worth testing; it does not yet establish the cost of automating it.”

**Weak:** “Agree? Comment YES.”

**Improved:** “What evidence would make you remove the review step?”

The second question requests material relevant to the argument. Use it only if the author wants that discussion and readers can answer without exposing confidential information. Self-selected replies remain contributions, not a representative survey. A completed insight, professional story or joke can end without a question. An offer can use its approved CTA without appending an unrelated discussion prompt.

## 7. Review the contract in both directions

Read from opening to ending: does the promised explanation arrive? Then read backward from the conclusion: which evidence supports it, and which paragraph supplies the reasoning? Label paragraph roles in a reverse outline and remove repetition that contributes no new function.

Check that numerical precision, vivid detail and narrative confidence have not strengthened the claim beyond its source. [LWT05: Hansen and Wänke, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20947772/) reports experiments in which concreteness affected perceived truth; only the author abstract was inspected here. Perceived truth is not factual verification. Reconcile each fact with its source after the voice edit.

The final review asks whether the intended reader can identify the point, its basis, its material limit and the offered next step. That is an editorial comprehension check. It is not evidence that the post will produce a particular platform or commercial outcome.
