# Publication pattern library

Version 2.0 · 10 November 2026. These are original editorial design patterns. They are selectable structures, not empirically proven reach formulas. Use [the architecture](../LINKEDIN-WRITING-ARCHITECTURE.md) and [the research record](RESEARCH-AND-REVIEW.md) for their evidence boundaries.

Select by the reader's question and the evidence available. A topic and media format do not determine the pattern. Where a case or personal story is unsupported, select a method, observation or working-question pattern instead.

## P01. Evidence-led insight

**Reader job:** Explain what one observation changes. **Use when:** There is an inspectable observation and a useful, bounded interpretation.

**Opening template:** [Observed result] changes how I would think about [specific decision].

**Sequence:**

1. Observation and its context
2. What the observation does establish
3. Reasoning: why it matters to the reader's decision
4. Alternative explanation or boundary
5. Practical implication.

**Evidence required:** Original source or dataset; population, period, denominator and comparison basis for numbers. An attributed external statement stays attributed.

**Reasoning check:** Explain the link between the observed difference and the decision. Do not replace an association with a causal mechanism.

**Conclusion template:** For [context], this supports [bounded action or interpretation]. It does not yet establish [material unresolved claim].

**Audience interaction:** Optionally ask what additional evidence would change the decision.

**Humor:** A brief recognition joke can sit outside the result sentence; keep the measurement literal.

**Stop or reroute:** If the observation cannot be located, hold the factual claim or write a question instead.

**Variation:** Result-first or question-first; one chart only when the chart answers the central question.

## P02. Founder decision story

**Reader job:** Make a professional judgment understandable. **Use when:** The founder can confirm a specific decision and the circumstances around it.

**Opening template:** At [specific moment], we had to choose between [real alternatives].

**Sequence:**

1. Moment and stakes
2. Goal and constraint at the time
3. Alternatives and decision
4. Reason for choosing with the information then available
5. Observed consequence
6. Present reflection and transfer limit.

**Evidence required:** Founder-confirmed account, date/context, actual alternatives, permission for shared details; a record for numerical outcomes.

**Reasoning check:** Show how the constraint and available evidence explain the choice. Separate the decision's rationale from what happened afterward.

**Conclusion template:** In that situation, [decision rule] mattered because [reason]. I would reconsider it when [condition].

**Audience interaction:** Ask which constraint changed a comparable decision, if discussion would add value.

**Humor:** Self-directed understatement about the process can fit an established founder voice.

**Stop or reroute:** Missing personal material is an interview gap, not an invitation to invent a scene.

**Variation:** Begin at the decision or at its consequence; avoid making hindsight sound inevitable.

## P03. Changed mind or mistake

**Reader job:** Explain a revision in professional understanding. **Use when:** There is a verifiable earlier belief, a disconfirming signal and an actual or proposed change.

**Opening template:** I used to assume [precise belief]. [Specific evidence] made me revisit it.

**Sequence:**

1. Earlier assumption and why it seemed reasonable
2. Action or test based on it
3. Disconfirming observation
4. Competing explanations checked
5. Revised understanding
6. Changed practice and remaining uncertainty.

**Evidence required:** Confirmed former position and incident; evidence for the disconfirming signal; distinguish an implemented change from an intention.

**Reasoning check:** The revision should follow from the evidence rather than from a moral lesson imposed afterward.

**Conclusion template:** I now use [narrower rule] in [context], while still checking [unresolved condition].

**Audience interaction:** Invite a counterexample or another condition that would change the rule.

**Humor:** Optional self-recognition; avoid self-deprecation that undermines a core capability claim.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not fabricate failure, vulnerability or a successful recovery to complete the arc.

**Variation:** A small corrected assumption can carry the post; a dramatic confession is unnecessary.

## P04. Workflow walkthrough

**Reader job:** Help the reader understand or attempt a process. **Use when:** A real or clearly labelled illustrative process can be made inspectable.

**Opening template:** Here is how [specific task] moves from [input] to [usable decision].

**Sequence:**

1. Task and user
2. Input and constraints
3. Steps with the reason for each
4. Output and acceptance check
5. Failure or exception
6. Adaptation or next attempt.

**Evidence required:** Actual process notes or an explicitly synthetic demonstration; current product/version evidence for capabilities.

**Reasoning check:** Connect every step to the problem it solves. Explain the human check where it exists.

**Conclusion template:** Use this sequence when [conditions]; change [step] if [constraint differs].

**Audience interaction:** Ask where the reader's workflow requires a different handoff.

**Humor:** A mild observation about process friction can help recognition, without blaming users.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not present an unrun example as a working deployment or measured saving.

**Variation:** Text, diagram, document or video according to how the process is best explained.

## P05. Diagnostic checklist

**Reader job:** Help a reader locate a problem or choose the next check. **Use when:** There are meaningful criteria with observable signs and consequences.

**Opening template:** Before you decide [X], check [specific risk or condition].

**Sequence:**

1. Decision and scope
2. Diagnostic questions or checks
3. What each answer implies
4. Small worked example
5. Priority or next action
6. Limit of the checklist.

**Evidence required:** Basis for the criteria; actual examples if claimed; label an original checklist as a proposed tool, not a validated diagnostic.

**Reasoning check:** Each check must change a decision. Items that merely sound sensible add length without diagnostic value.

**Conclusion template:** Start with [highest-impact check] when [condition]; escalate or investigate if [signal].

**Audience interaction:** Invite a missing check from people who have used the process.

**Humor:** Optional light label for a recognisable process habit, not for a person.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not imply completeness, legal assurance or measured accuracy without supporting evidence.

**Variation:** Three checks are not mandatory; use the smallest set that covers the stated decision.

## P06. Research translation

**Reader job:** Explain what a study means for a practical question. **Use when:** The underlying research and its limitations can be inspected.

**Opening template:** [Study/source] examined [question]. Here is the part relevant to [reader decision].

**Sequence:**

1. Practical question
2. Study population, design and comparison
3. Material result
4. What the result supports
5. Transfer limits and alternative interpretation
6. Practical implication or next test.

**Evidence required:** Primary paper/report, publication status, methods and result actually accessed; mark abstract-only access and any missing details.

**Reasoning check:** Separate what the researchers measured from the new professional context. A survey preference is not a demonstrated sales effect.

**Conclusion template:** This gives us a reason to test [application], within [scope]; it does not settle [broader claim].

**Audience interaction:** Ask for a relevant replication or field constraint, rather than asking readers to agree with the study.

**Humor:** Keep jokes away from quoted findings, source attribution and statistical claims.

**Stop or reroute:** If the method is inaccessible, narrow the summary to the accessible evidence and retain the gap.

**Variation:** A short source note can lead to an article/newsletter for the full reasoning.

## P07. Qualified counterposition

**Reader job:** Improve a common decision rule. **Use when:** A precisely stated conventional view has a material boundary or overlooked alternative.

**Opening template:** [Common advice] is useful when [condition]. It becomes less useful when [specific condition].

**Sequence:**

1. Fair account of the common view
2. Where it works
3. Evidence or example exposing a boundary
4. Alternative explanation or rule
5. Strong counterargument
6. Conditional recommendation.

**Evidence required:** Traceable formulation of the position or a clearly labelled generalisation; examples and sources supporting the boundary.

**Reasoning check:** Challenge the rule's scope, mechanism or evidence. Disagreement alone does not establish the opposite.

**Conclusion template:** Choose [A] when [criterion]; choose [B] when [different criterion].

**Audience interaction:** Invite a circumstance that breaks the proposed distinction.

**Humor:** Gentle irony about an overbroad slogan may fit; avoid mocking named people.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not invent a consensus, cherry-pick a weak opponent or force false balance.

**Variation:** Use a comparison table or a short case if it clarifies the conditions.

## P08. Measured case or customer proof

**Reader job:** Let a buyer assess a documented result. **Use when:** An authorised case has a defined result and inspectable measurement context.

**Opening template:** For [defined case], [metric] moved from [baseline] to [result] during [period].

**Sequence:**

1. Case and initial condition
2. Intervention and concurrent changes
3. Measurement method and result
4. What can be attributed and what cannot
5. Practical explanation
6. Who this case may or may not apply to.

**Evidence required:** Permission, source, baseline, denominator, timeframe, measurement method and relevant costs; causal design if causal wording is used.

**Reasoning check:** Distinguish outcome, mechanism hypothesis and attribution. Show relevant concurrent changes.

**Conclusion template:** This case demonstrates [observed result] under [conditions]; further evidence is needed for [generalisation].

**Audience interaction:** Offer the measurement method or invite a comparable case if permitted.

**Humor:** Usually low. Keep customer experience and result statements clear and respectful.

**Stop or reroute:** No approved case or measurement means no outcome claim; reroute to P04 or P05.

**Variation:** A customer quote is evidence of that person's reported experience, not independent proof of every performance claim.

## P09. Build update or release

**Reader job:** Explain a concrete change and its significance. **Use when:** A release, milestone, experiment or learning update is documented.

**Opening template:** We changed [specific thing]. Here is the problem it is meant to address.

**Sequence:**

1. What changed and release state
2. User problem or decision behind it
3. Demonstration or change evidence
4. Trade-off or remaining limitation
5. Who should use or test it
6. Next step without an unsupported roadmap promise.

**Evidence required:** Release record, current capability, demonstration conditions and approved availability wording.

**Reasoning check:** Explain why the change matters to the user. Shipping is not evidence of adoption or value.

**Conclusion template:** For [user/task], the change enables [verified capability]. We still need to learn [open outcome].

**Audience interaction:** Ask for feedback on a specific task or failure condition, when the team can use it.

**Humor:** Light process humor may work if the feature and availability remain unambiguous.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not announce an unshipped feature as available or imply a completed customer outcome.

**Variation:** A progress update can contain a failure and a next decision instead of a success milestone.

## P10. Comparison or review

**Reader job:** Support a choice between alternatives. **Use when:** The alternatives can be assessed against the same relevant criteria.

**Opening template:** For [task], the useful difference between [A] and [B] is [criterion].

**Sequence:**

1. Reader task and constraints
2. Alternatives and version/date
3. Common criteria and test or evidence
4. Trade-offs with examples
5. Conditions that change the preference
6. Recommendation for a defined user.

**Evidence required:** Comparable sources or an actual described test; costs and versions where material; relationships or incentives disclosed where relevant.

**Reasoning check:** Tie the recommendation to criteria. A preferred result on one task does not establish universal superiority.

**Conclusion template:** Choose [A] for [condition]; [B] is a better fit when [condition], based on [evidence].

**Audience interaction:** Ask which constraint or use case the comparison missed.

**Humor:** A concise analogy can make a trade-off memorable without replacing the comparison.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not call a desk review hands-on or compare mismatched baselines.

**Variation:** A review of one tool can compare it with the current manual process.

## P11. Working question or discussion

**Reader job:** Collect useful perspectives on an unresolved decision. **Use when:** The author can define a real question and explain what is already known.

**Opening template:** I am deciding [specific question]. The unresolved part is [trade-off].

**Sequence:**

1. Decision and context
2. What is known
3. Options and trade-off
4. Author's provisional view
5. Precise request for relevant experience.

**Evidence required:** Context and stated known facts; label the author's view as provisional.

**Reasoning check:** Explain why the requested experience could change the decision. Keep the question answerable without disclosing confidential details.

**Conclusion template:** I would change [provisional view] if [specific evidence or condition].

**Audience interaction:** One focused question; permit nuanced answers and no compulsory reply.

**Humor:** Recognition can lower stiffness, but avoid making the question seem insincere.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not invent a dilemma solely to solicit comments or report replies as a representative survey.

**Variation:** A poll is optional and remains self-selected feedback.

## P12. Offer or invitation

**Reader job:** Help a relevant reader decide whether to take a next step. **Use when:** The offer, eligibility, scope and next action are current and authorised.

**Opening template:** For [specific reader] dealing with [specific problem], we offer [bounded service or resource].

**Sequence:**

1. Reader and problem
2. What is offered and included
3. How it works
4. Available evidence or transparent evidence gap
5. Fit and meaningful limits
6. One clear next action.

**Evidence required:** Approved offer and delivery capability; approved price/availability if included; substantiated case claims only.

**Reasoning check:** Explain the connection between the service and the reader's problem. Do not substitute urgency or popularity for fit.

**Conclusion template:** If [fit condition], [approved next action].

**Audience interaction:** Use a direct, relevant action. A discussion question is not required.

**Humor:** Optional, low risk; never obscure price, scope, commitments or eligibility.

**Stop or reroute:** No invented scarcity, clients, guaranteed outcomes or unapproved offer changes.

**Variation:** An event/resource invitation may use the same pattern with a clear purpose and recipient.

## P13. Focused professional observation

**Reader job:** Make one useful distinction memorable. **Use when:** A small, bounded insight is sufficient and does not need a full story.

**Opening template:** [Concrete observation or distinction].

**Sequence:**

1. Observation
2. Specific illustration or explanation
3. Why the distinction matters
4. Compact takeaway.

**Evidence required:** Source for factual assertions; a labelled illustration for a conceptual distinction. Personal observations remain bounded.

**Reasoning check:** Show the practical difference the distinction makes. Avoid upgrading a memorable line into a universal law.

**Conclusion template:** The next time [situation], check [useful distinction].

**Audience interaction:** A complete closing thought is often enough.

**Humor:** Dry understatement or a restrained analogy can carry the voice.

**Stop or reroute:** Do not pad a short insight with fabricated experience, generic lessons or an unrelated CTA.

**Variation:** One paragraph or several, according to the development of the thought.

## P14. Professional humor or recognisable scene

**Reader job:** Create recognition around a work situation. **Use when:** The author has a suitable voice and the joke can be understood without misleading factual claims.

**Opening template:** [Recognisable situation] with [a small, intelligible reversal].

**Sequence:**

1. Clear situation
2. Expectation
3. Benign incongruity or escalation
4. Payoff
5. Optional professional observation.

**Evidence required:** Real anecdotes require confirmation and permission. Fictional dialogue or scenes must not impersonate an actual client or colleague.

**Reasoning check:** Humor need not prove a thesis. If a practical claim follows, support it separately.

**Conclusion template:** End on the payoff or add one modest insight; avoid attaching a moral to every joke.

**Audience interaction:** Recognition can be the complete response. Do not force a sales CTA after the punchline.

**Humor:** The main job; use process or self-directed targets, with cultural and reputational judgment.

**Stop or reroute:** Avoid humiliating clients, confidential incidents, harmful stereotypes or jokes that blur evidence.

**Variation:** A clearly illustrative scene, short dialogue, dry observation or original metaphor; do not imitate a reference creator's distinctive wording.

## Pattern selection examples

A result with no causal design can use P01 with qualified interpretation. A founder-confirmed decision can use P02. Missing founder memories should route to P04, P05, P11 or P13 rather than a fabricated P02/P03. An approved service can use P12 even when it has no customer-outcome evidence, provided it explains the offer honestly. A joke can use P14 without pretending that amusement establishes product value.
