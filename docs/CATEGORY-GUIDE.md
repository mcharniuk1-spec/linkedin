# A practical category system for founder-led publications

Use separate labels for **subject**, **editorial job** and **format**. A video is a format, not a topic. A founder story can teach a positioning lesson; its main topic is the decision the reader should understand. Assign one primary topic and only useful secondary topics.

| Primary topic | Include when the main point is… | A useful original founder publication |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning and messaging | Who the product serves, its category, alternatives, differentiation or wording. | Explain why you narrowed one buyer segment, using the actual trade-off and changed wording. |
| Buyer research | How buyers decide, objections, evaluation, trust or learning from customers. | Describe a real objection and what evidence changed your understanding. |
| Founder building | Founder decisions, milestones, mistakes, constraints or product/company development. | Show one decision, the constraint, rejected option and lesson. |
| AI product education | How an AI product/workflow works, its limits and practical use. | Walk through an authorized example with input, output, failure case and buyer consequence. |
| Sales and outbound | Prospecting, conversations, qualification, pipeline or commercial execution. | Explain one sales decision or useful question, without promising a guaranteed outcome. |
| Demand generation | Market awareness, distribution, channels, demand creation/capture and measurement. | Show how you chose one channel and what your actual measurement can establish. |
| Content and personal brand | Creating, reviewing, distributing and sustaining professional content. | Explain how a real customer conversation became a useful post, preserving confidentiality. |
| Leadership and team | Hiring, culture, management, collaboration and operating habits. | Present an operating tension and a concrete behavior your team changed. |
| Customer proof | A customer example, result, testimonial or evidence is the main message. | Use a permissioned case with baseline, timeframe, denominator, source and limits. |
| Career and general work | Job searches, individual professional growth or workplace advice. | Use selectively for personal connection; do not substitute it for buyer-specific expertise. |
| Events and community | An event, community contribution, collaboration or participation is central. | Share a useful lesson from the event before the invitation or attendance update. |
| Adjacent humor | A joke, parody, meme or entertaining situation carries the post. | Make a recognizable buyer/work situation entertaining; keep it distinct from product evidence. |

The eight editorial jobs are: teach a method, challenge a belief, show proof, build in public, tell a story, announce an offer, start a discussion and entertain. Choose the dominant job. A sales CTA does not make every educational post an offer announcement.

Format comes from the actual exposed media: text/external link, image, document preview, video or a combination. The media-role explanation says what the creative adds: evidence, explanation, face/personality, product demonstration, diagram, illustration or mood. A mismatch is useful evidence too. Do not call a decorative portrait proof of the post's numerical claims.

## How to use the qualification table

- **Core:** direct or transferable buyer/business insight for the service. This does not certify factual accuracy or performance.
- **Adjacent:** useful personal-brand, style or community example with a weaker buyer connection.
- **Hold:** interpretation depends on missing media, ambiguous professional context, unsupported critical claims or unresolved source issues.
- **Exclude:** the current candidate does not serve the reference objective. Keep its audit record; do not use it to pad a top-performer list.

Read the statistical column independently. “Strong sample reference” means an upper-band observed reaction count in a sufficiently large comparable sample. “Insufficient comparable sample” can apply to a thoughtful, relevant post. It should not be translated as “bad content.” “Growth observed” is not a trend declaration.

For your future editorial plan, select a few buyer-relevant subjects, vary the editorial job, and choose media that serves that job. Build an original evidence-backed version using your own business experiences. The adaptation guidance is a creative hypothesis to test, not a template to copy or a promised route to reach or sales.
