19 October 2026. Proposed service offer for owner review. The audience and approved hero/VP are owner instructions. The packaging, production allocation and differentiation below are hypotheses; no buyer acceptance or delivery economics is established.

## Keep the approved front door

**Make the right buyers care about you and what you’re building.**

We help Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders turn their story and the business they’re building into LinkedIn content that attracts the right buyers, earns their trust and gives them a reason to buy.

**Book an intro call**

Audience line: **For Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders · 10–50 people · Seed to Series B**

The narrow segment gives the editorial team a useful focus. It does not prove that every founder in the segment has the same buying problem. Research should distinguish technical founders seeking product comprehension, founders building a repeatable sales motion, and founders who need a consistent executive voice alongside an existing marketing team.

## The service position

**Founder-led LinkedIn content and campaigns, built from the company you’re actually building.**

Your product decisions, customer conversations and point of view give buyers something useful to understand. We turn that material into a clear position, consistent writing and visual content. Then we connect publishing, paid amplification and measurement around the same buyer story.

The proposed editorial lens is broader than AI safety, AI news or a personal founder diary. It connects founder judgment to a buyer's decision: the problem, product trade-off, operating constraint, implementation experience, credible evidence and next step. These are topic directions, not facts about a client before discovery.

Do not claim that interviews, voice matching, a knowledge base or an AI-assisted process are unique. Multiple workbook competitors already offer them. The candidate difference is the combination of specific AI-SaaS product understanding, a source-linked founder voice, coherent written/visual/video production, and feedback from real buyer conversations. Validate that combination with a permissioned sample and paid pilot.

## How the work runs

1. **Understand the business.** Review your product, buyers, existing evidence and point of view. Agree on the message and practical scope.
2. **Capture the useful material.** Interview the founder and collect only approved documents, recordings and examples. Build the voice and story bank.
3. **Create and review.** Write, design and prepare video around specific buyer questions. You approve the exact content before release.
4. **Publish and learn.** Use the authorized publishing workflow. Review buyer signals, sales feedback and delivery effort to decide what to develop next.

Founder input is part of the product. Do not promise five minutes a week or zero involvement before a pilot measures the actual interview, review and correction work.

## Review copy: three agency LinkedIn posts

Drafts for this company’s owner approval. They use the proposed offer, not invented customer outcomes. No publication occurred.

### Post 1 — The product decision is the story

Your product roadmap is not a LinkedIn content calendar.

But the decisions behind it can be a strong place to start.

Why did you choose one integration over another?
What did a customer need that the original product missed?
Which trade-off do buyers need to understand before they can evaluate your AI product?

Those details give people a way to understand your judgment, the problem you solve and the business you’re building.

That is the material we help Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders turn into useful LinkedIn content.

A practical starting point: take one recent product decision and explain what it changes for the buyer. Keep the confidential details out. Keep the reasoning in.

### Post 2 — A good draft needs a source

A founder’s content should sound like the founder.

It should also be something they can stand behind.

Before writing, we want to know:

• Did the founder actually have this experience?
• Is the product claim true today?
• Can the customer example be shared?
• Does the number have a source, date and useful context?

Voice matters. So does the material underneath it.

For an AI SaaS company, that material might be an implementation decision, a buyer question, a limitation you chose to explain or a workflow you can demonstrate.

Start with something true and useful. Then make it clear enough to read in the feed.

### Post 3 — Connect the content to the buyer

What should a buyer understand after reading your next post?

That is a useful question to ask before choosing the format.

If the idea is a product trade-off, a short explanation may be enough.
If the process has several steps, a diagram or document may make it clearer.
If the product needs to be seen, a focused demonstration may do the job.

The same question should guide the profile, the visual and the next step.

We help Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders connect those pieces: positioning, writing, design, video and LinkedIn campaigns around a buyer-relevant story.

## Carousel script and visual brief

Title: **One product decision. Several useful content angles.**

1. The decision: what changed, in plain language.
2. The buyer problem: what made the decision necessary.
3. The trade-off: what the team chose and what it gave up.
4. The evidence: a permissioned example, demonstration or clearly sourced observation.
5. The buyer implication: when this matters and when it does not.
6. The next question: what a buyer should ask when evaluating an approach like this.

Use the design agents’ selected tokens, type scale and diagram rules. Use a fictional placeholder product only if the artifact is clearly labeled a specimen. Keep metrics and product UI out until the client supplies verified material. Do not imitate competitor creative.
