# LinkedIn writing architecture v2

Version: 2.0 · 10 November 2026 · task OUTSP-45 · status: delivered for founder review.

## Purpose and navigation

This is the current composition method for LinkedIn publications in this project. It extends the earlier general writing methodology and the project's founder-writing workflow. It specifies how an idea becomes a post: publication type, opening, evidence, explanation, conclusion, voice and an appropriate reader response. It does not establish a posting cadence or a formula for reach.

The agency audience is Europe-based B2B AI SaaS founders, 10–50 people, Seed–Series B. A client publication must use that client's own reader, product, evidence and voice brief; it must not automatically inherit the agency audience. ## 1. Five separate decisions

Keep the topic, reader job, publication pattern, media format and voice separate. "AI" is a topic; "help a buyer evaluate human review requirements" is a job; "workflow walkthrough" is a pattern; "document carousel" is a format. Dry humor is a voice choice. These labels answer different questions.

Choose the reader job first. A post should leave the relevant reader better able to understand, evaluate, decide, attempt, remember or discuss something specific. The intended commercial role may be awareness, consideration, qualification or an offer. That role does not determine how every paragraph is written.

Use one dominant pattern per post. A short example or counterargument can sit inside it, but combining a founder memoir, research review, checklist and service pitch usually creates competing promises. Split the material when each part answers a different reader question.

## 2. Start with an evidence packet

Before selecting a dramatic opening, write the strongest supportable point in ordinary language. List what is known, who knows it, where it is recorded and what remains unresolved. A claim can be an observation, an attributed source statement, an inference, a recommendation, a forecast or a personal recollection. Preserve that distinction in the final wording.

For an actual founder story, collect: the situation and date, the goal, a constraint, considered alternatives, the decision, its stated reason, the observed consequence and the founder's present interpretation. Record which details are confirmed by the founder, which have a document or measurement, and which are unavailable. Do not fill a missing interview with a plausible memory.

For a product claim, collect the task, input, output, version or date, conditions of the demonstration, human intervention and a relevant failure or limit. A screenshot can demonstrate a state; it does not by itself establish frequency, reliability, savings or causality.

For a numerical result, retain the numerator, denominator, unit, period, population, source and comparison basis. A useful number may be a descriptive observation. Its precision does not automatically make the explanation causal.

When material is missing, choose a lower-burden pattern. A proposed checklist can be useful without becoming a fabricated customer case. A question can remain open without becoming a confident answer. This is a routing decision, not merely a disclaimer appended after drafting.

## 3. Compose an argument or a story

### Argument route

Use the following functional sequence when the post's main job is to explain or persuade:

1. **Relevance:** establish the concrete situation or decision.
2. **Point:** state a bounded answer or position.
3. **Evidence:** provide the observation, source, worked example or actual demonstration that bears on it.
4. **Reasoning:** explain why this material changes the answer.
5. **Boundary:** name a condition, alternative explanation or missing piece that changes the conclusion.
6. **Payoff:** state the practical implication in proportion to the evidence.
7. **Response, if useful:** give one appropriate next action or a genuine question.

This is a set of functions, not a mandatory seven-paragraph layout. A short post can combine functions. A research post may need more than one evidence-and-reasoning unit. The opening can present the result first, provided the body supplies its context and support.

### Story route

Use the following sequence when the reader needs to understand a professional experience:

1. **A concrete moment:** what was happening and why it mattered.
2. **Goal and constraint:** what the person was trying to do, with the information available then.
3. **Choice:** the alternatives and the actual decision.
4. **Consequence:** what was observed, including mixed or unfinished outcomes.
5. **Reflection:** how the author interprets the experience now.
6. **Transfer:** the bounded lesson another professional can use.

Keep the knowledge of the person in the scene separate from the knowledge of the narrator today. Avoid presenting an uncertain decision as an inevitable success. A professional story can end in a better question, a revised practice or a still-open trade-off.

## 4. Write the hook as a promise the body can keep

A hook establishes why this reader should continue. It can name a difficult choice, show a concrete observation, expose an assumption, ask a precise question or introduce an intelligible surprise. It should contain enough context for the reader to recognise the subject.

Write the core point and its support before producing opening variants. Then draft two or three different openings for the same supported content. Compare relevance, specificity, truthfulness and the information withheld. Do not choose an opening solely because it sounds more dramatic.

The opening creates a payoff obligation. If it promises a decision rule, the body must supply that rule. If it announces a result, the body must explain the result's scope. If it introduces a scene, the scene must matter to the conclusion. A curiosity gap is useful only if resolving it gives the reader something worthwhile.

There is no fixed number of opening characters in this method. LinkedIn's interface, device and format affect what is initially visible. Preview the actual publication when that becomes part of an authorised publishing task; this methodology update has not performed a native preview.

## 5. Give each paragraph a job

An explanatory paragraph normally has one dominant contribution. It may introduce a claim, present material, interpret it, address an exception or carry a decision forward. In a story, it may establish a scene, reveal a constraint, show action or shift the narrator's perspective.

Use a reverse outline after drafting. Label each paragraph by function, then ask whether the sequence makes sense without the decorative wording. Repetition, unsupported leaps and a second competing thesis become easier to see.

The local argument unit is **claim → relevant material → explanation of the link → consequence or limit**. Not every unit needs four sentences. What matters is that the inference is visible. "Therefore" cannot create a causal connection that the evidence does not support.

Use short paragraphs when they improve pacing or make a change of thought visible. Preserve sentences that belong together. An isolated one-line sentence can create emphasis; repeating that device throughout a post can make all lines compete for emphasis.

## 6. Make the conclusion earned

The conclusion should answer the opening's promise and state what the reader can reasonably take away. It should not introduce a stronger, unsupported claim. In a short post, one useful closing sentence may be enough.

Choose one ending from the job of the post: a bounded conclusion, a decision rule, a concrete application, an acknowledged uncertainty, an invitation to contribute relevant experience, or a direct offer. A finished thought does not require a question after it.

When inviting discussion, ask for information that would extend the subject: the condition under which the rule failed, a different workflow, a missing comparison or a concrete decision. Give the reader enough context to answer. Avoid artificial consensus prompts, forced binary choices and requests to perform an engagement action as a substitute for contributing.

For an offer, make the intended buyer, scope, relevant evidence and next step clear. An offer is a legitimate publication type. A low reaction median in a selected historical sample is insufficient grounds to prohibit it. Evaluate the offer against its actual purpose and available outcomes.

## 7. Add interest before adding decoration

Interest can come from a recognisable professional tension, an unexpected but supported result, a concrete detail, a useful distinction, an inspectable process or a consequence the reader cares about. These are content decisions. They do not depend on exaggerated language.

Prefer details that change understanding. A date, rejected option, input example or error condition may reveal the decision process. An incidental personal detail can create atmosphere, but it should not obscure the point or pretend to supply evidence.

Use analogies to explain one relationship and state where the analogy stops working if that matters. Comparisons can aid understanding while also hiding differences. A cinematic reference, joke or metaphor should remain comprehensible to the intended audience and optional to understanding the argument.

Humor is an optional layer. Match it to the author's existing voice and the stakes of the subject. Keep quantitative evidence, source attributions and limitations unambiguous. The companion humor guide distinguishes supported findings from editorial extrapolation.

## 8. Adapt the architecture to the medium

| Medium | Architecture decision | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Text post | Keep the central promise, evidence and payoff readable as one unit; trim secondary arguments first. | Check current platform limits and the actual caption before publication. |
| Single image plus caption | Decide whether the image explains, demonstrates, compares or illustrates. Put essential meaning in accessible text too. | A decorative or fictional image is not customer proof; source/limit labels must remain legible. |
| Document/carousel | Give each panel a new function: question, context, evidence, mechanism, boundary, application as needed. | Panel count is a design choice, not a performance rule; caption and panels must make compatible claims. |
| Short video | Use a spoken opening, visible action or example, one explanation and a clear close. | Visual evidence must match the spoken claim; use accurate captions. |
| Article/newsletter | Add navigation, fuller evidence, competing explanations and references where the subject needs them. | The series promise and each issue's question must be explicit. |
| Poll | Use neutral wording and meaningful options for conversation or exploratory feedback. | A self-selected audience poll is not a representative market survey. |

No format inherits a guaranteed distribution advantage from this method. The available historical corpus combines different authors, topics, publication ages and audiences. Format is selected for the explanatory job, then evaluated in context.

## 9. Review in separate passes

**Structure pass:** identify the dominant reader job, pattern and argument or story sequence. Check that every paragraph contributes and the opening is paid off.

**Evidence pass:** reconcile facts, quotations, numbers and modalities with the claim ledger. Check source independence, conditions and alternatives. A second agent agreeing is not independent factual evidence.

**Voice pass:** preserve the author's stance, vocabulary, rhythm and level of uncertainty. Remove generic ceremony and redundant phrasing without inventing personal experience or erasing technical distinctions.

**Audience and interest pass:** check professional relevance, specificity, cognitive load, examples, pacing and optional humor. A simulated persona can reveal a possible reading problem; it is not a representative audience test.

**Format pass:** verify caption/panel consistency, accessible descriptions, source legibility, word or character limits and exact protected wording. Design and native publishing checks are separate tasks.

Return factual changes to the evidence pass. The critical failure list is short: fabricated source or experience, unsupported causal promise, altered quotation or number, contradiction between caption and visual, and unresolved permission for private material. These failures cannot be averaged away by a high style score.

## 11. Completion criteria for a publication architecture

A draft structure is ready for author review when the reader job and pattern are explicit; the central claim has a matching evidence plan; each paragraph has a contribution; the reasoning connects evidence to conclusion; meaningful limits remain visible; the hook is fulfilled; the ending serves the job; and any first-person memory, joke, quote or product claim has the appropriate source and review status.

The structure can be complete while a publishable post remains blocked by missing founder material. Record that state honestly. The present task delivers methodology and current-post skeleton updates for review. It makes no live publication, client-performance or audience-validation claim.
