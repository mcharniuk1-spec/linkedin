# Discovery, source depth and cadence

12 November 2026. Apply this note when planning a founder programme or writing about OutSpoken's intro call. It refines the current buyer/voice brief; it does not replace source cards or change the tested skills.

1. Identify the account's purpose: founder judgment, practitioner teaching, recurring editorial analysis, or company/buying-group explanation. Verify the actual role; do not infer it from a parser's Person/Organization type.
2. On the 45-minute call, ask for the product, buyer problem, one real choice/example, factual reviewer and available source/review time. Record what can be public. Ask deeper follow-ups only when a missing fact would change a draft.
3. Choose a provisional cadence from distinct usable material, useful formats, founder/agency capacity and review constraints. One or two to three posts per week are possible test hypotheses, not findings or a universal prescription.
4. Write a hook that asks a concrete buyer question, explain the situation and choice, show the mechanism or worked example, state relevant limits, and end with a useful next action or answerable question. Do not invent the founder's experience to create a story.
5. Let the visual explain the decision: an annotated example, comparison, ordered worksheet or agenda. Use numbers only with a clear denominator and source, or label them as illustrative planning values. Keep the approved brand palette, type and spacing. Avoid decorative filler.
6. Keep free-output scope, timing and correction limits conditional until OUTSP-14/84 owner decisions are recorded. Preserve the exact approved CTA. A draft and local gallery are not social publication.

The frozen 279-post/61-account sample is selected and incomplete for author histories. It does not prove optimal posting frequency, reach, conversion or current market trends. The 198 reobservations are snapshots, not new posts.
