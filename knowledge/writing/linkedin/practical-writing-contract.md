# Practical publication contract for OutSpoken

## Reader-facing output

Every post should answer a specific audience question and offer a useful next action. Use a strong hook, a real situation or clearly labelled worked example, an explained method and an earned conclusion. A list should tell the reader both what to do and why that step matters.

The author is Max; the company repost adds a service implication or useful method. General professional judgment can shape the perspective. Do not insert explicit ArchFlow/GitHub/project-history references, invented career episodes, client outcomes or emotions. The supplied feedback permits an OutSpoken-centered perspective; it does not create missing facts.

The current practical edition covers the service flow, content strategy, interview, profile, tool choices, first message, reply, public repurposing case, buying group, reporting, design, development, team review and offer. It includes founder, designer, developer, manager/business-owner and non-technical team perspectives.

## Concrete structure

1. **Hook:** a specific question, tension or result the body can explain. Avoid a universal secret or unsupported market claim.
2. **Situation:** who is trying to do what, and where the work becomes difficult? Use evidence or label an illustrative scenario.
3. **Solution:** provide a usable method, template, question, tool workflow or worked comparison. Show actual input/output when helpful.
4. **Reasoning:** explain why the step addresses that problem. Keep any material condition beside the claim it qualifies.
5. **Conclusion:** state what the reader can do next; ask a genuine question or offer a relevant conversation. Do not force a sales pitch into every post.

Research audit detail belongs in the source/claim ledger. Keep the caption’s necessary attribution and material context, but do not make every paragraph a disclaimer. This changes presentation, not factual standards.

## Visual signal structure

- First attention: one clear buyer question or useful object, not a decorative dashboard.
- Main explanation: a worked flow, meaningful grouping, comparison, annotated example, checklist or response structure.
- Emphasis: use weight, scale, spacing and the current marker accent to help the reader find the decision. A structural number is not a result metric.
- Numbers: show the share/comparison the source supports, including year/population or case context. Do not invent own-account growth. Source and statistical details remain accessible in the ledger.
- Objects/scenes: represent the actual document, task, conversation or setting. Use original native diagrams and owned/cleared assets. A generated scene cannot become evidence of a meeting or customer result.
- Caption relation: the visual carries part of the explanation; the caption adds the story, reasons and application. Review both as one revision.

Use currentFigma foundations257:2: porcelain, navy, marker, Young Serif stand-in for Zodiak and Mona Sans. Prior Archivo/Fraunces/R13 artwork remains history for this edition.

## Formatting and evidence

Feed exports use short coherent paragraphs, bullets/numbering where useful and a clear ending. Bold is real typography in the visuals and review page. LinkedIn documents native bold for articles; a feed formatter is a separate vendor workflow. Do not paste Markdown asterisks as if native bold were established. Optional formatted variants need actual native readability/copy checks before release; no Unicode styling is required.

Monthly and weekly questions establish direction. A separate post only needs additional founder questions when the story, claim, product behavior or stance is missing. The agent should research and prefill the rest.
