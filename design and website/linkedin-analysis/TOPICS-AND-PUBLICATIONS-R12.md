# LinkedIn analysis and publication drafts for the OUTSPOKEN profile (R12, 2 November 2026; proposed)

## 1. Source and method
Source: the repository's own reference sample (`reports/20261031/`, collection and analysis described in `reports/20261031/README.md`): **279 reviewed posts from 61 accounts**, counters captured 20 to 22 October 2026, publication dates 2022 to 2026. Statistics are **descriptive medians of observed reactions**. Verification of the report: 240 collector tests, 21 export and boundary tests and independent statistical checks passed. **Not observed:** impressions, reach, video views, clicks, saves, conversions. Author size, post age and topic differ between rows, so no causal effect of a format or topic can be claimed. Ten posts account for 33.04 percent of all observed reactions.

## 3. Topic selection for OUTSPOKEN (reasoned, not guaranteed)
1. **Personal brand and positioning** are the studio's own subject and led the sample (258 and 179). Own the topic with a method post.
2. **Founder building** (203.5) fits the founder-led angle: real decisions and trade-offs. Use "tell a story" and "challenge a belief" purposes, which led or sat in the upper half.
3. **Avoid** leading with offers (67) or generic demand-generation talk (26): they sat at the bottom, and the studio should earn trust before it announces anything.
4. **Format:** images and documents (carousels) outperformed text and video in medians. Use a carousel for the method and the data posts, one strong image for a belief to challenge; keep video for demonstrations where voice adds information.
5. **Voice:** personal profiles outperformed organization pages in the sample. Publish from the founders' personal profiles and use the company page for workflows, product facts and permissioned proof.

## 4. Publication drafts (images in `design and website/assets/public/assets/publications-r12/`; generator `scripts/make_publications.py`)
All numbers on the slides come from the data above. No client, result, quote or real person is claimed. Wording is proposed; the founders approve before anything is published. Nothing was posted.

### P1: carousel, 7 slides (document upload: `p1-method-carousel.pdf`): "One product decision. One post buyers can use."
Purpose: teach a method. Caption:

```
Most founder posts explain a feature. Buyers are asking about a decision.

Here is the five-step method we use to turn one product decision into one post a buyer can use:
1. Start with a decision, not a feature.
2. Name the buyer's question.
3. Show the trade-off.
4. Add one piece of evidence.
5. End with a lesson to use.

Why this order: in a sample of 279 reviewed LinkedIn posts from 61 accounts, personal-brand topics had a median of 258 reactions and positioning topics 179 (descriptive; author, age and topic differ, and no reach data was available).

If you want this voice on your profile, book an intro call.
#founderledmarketing #B2BSaaS
```

We read 279 LinkedIn posts from 61 accounts so you do not have to guess.

## 5. Suggested rhythm and measurement
Publish P1 first (method, own topic), then P3 (proof with limits), then P2 (belief). Two to three posts a week from the founders' profiles beat a daily calendar of filler. Measure per post: reactions, comments, qualified replies and conversations that name the buyer question; compare only like with like (same author, similar age). Report what the authorized data shows and nothing else. Re-run the collection report after four weeks and compare the medians of the studio's own posts with this sample as a reference, not as a target.
