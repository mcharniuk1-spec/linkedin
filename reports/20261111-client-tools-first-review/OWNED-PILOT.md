## T03/T04: actual-company story output

**Reader job:** recognize when an explanatory visual serves the production process instead of the buyer. **Pattern:** a real revision/learning story. **Evidence:** the two linked dated releases and the owner's direct feedback. **Reasoning:** a chart or label is useful only when it helps the reader answer the intended question. **Limit:** this revision does not establish audience response or conversion improvement.

**Caption draft**

Our earlier LinkedIn publication set explained too much about how the posts were made—and too little about the buyer’s question.

The panels had founder-note labels, page counters and diagrams. The feedback was direct: the ideas were not practical enough, and the visuals were weak.

We revised all ten concepts. Out went the production labels and decorative arrows. In came clearer comparisons, annotated wording and questions a reader could use to examine a product decision.

The change gave us a more useful review question: what can someone understand or decide after seeing this panel?

That question applies to the caption too. A strong opening creates an expectation; the explanation and evidence need to fulfil it. A feature list or a polished sentence can still leave the important choice unexplained.

We have a revised set, not proof that it will perform better. The next step is to check how readers interpret it.

Before adding another visual element, write down the question it needs to answer.

**Visual handoff:** use one actual old/new wording comparison and the reader question. Keep the editorial text native, readable and in the current Young Serif/Mona Sans/porcelain/navy system. A production screenshot would need a clear role and readable crop; it must not become background filler. No new image is claimed rendered for this rehearsal.
