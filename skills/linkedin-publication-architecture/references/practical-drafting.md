# Practical drafting from a source packet

## Prepare the right brief

For a month, establish the commercial job, buyer situations and decisions, source material, founder position, proof gaps, formats, conversation path, capacity and learning question. For a week, choose the actual buyer questions, usable inputs, owner/reviewer, format and next action. For one post, narrow to the reader question, point, source/scene, example, takeaway and ending. Use a supplied template when present. Prefill what current evidence and prior answers already establish; ask only unresolved decisions or facts. Do not request a product version or extra biography for a general recommendation unless it changes the actual claim or requested visual. A calendar is not a strategy, and a recurring brief is not a reason to repeat answered questions.

Read the current author/buyer/offer brief. When a source-bound knowledge base is supplied, select relevant writing cards and a visual card only when useful. Follow each card back to its evidence modality, date, limits and human-required fields. Transfer the function, not an author's wording, biography, image or results. A source packet can be sufficient without a knowledge database or vector tool.

## Give the reader something usable

Answer the basic question early. Build the body around an actual decision, a worked example, a tool with inputs/steps/output, a useful comparison, or a checklist whose checks change the recommendation. Explain why the suggested action helps and when it may fail. Do not substitute technical audit terminology, a vague promise or an impressive figure for the answer.

One useful shape is hook → recognizable situation → specific approach/example → reason → earned conclusion or next step. It is an option, not a mandatory story formula. State the tool's job before naming the vendor; distinguish a documented feature from a connected or tested integration. Link a service to the relevant buyer situation using its approved scope. An offer description does not prove completed client results.

Use first person for an actual position or confirmed experience. If an event is missing, write a professional recommendation, explain a labelled illustrative situation or ask a focused source question. Never create autobiographical detail to meet a story quota. Concrete judgment and natural rhythm can make a post human without a dramatic confession. Vary openings and endings across a batch; repeated “I want” or “I would” framing can obscure the useful point. Humor stays optional and must not make the evidence ambiguous.

## Keep the caption readable and the proof inspectable

Put lineage, missing-field audits and process notes in the review packet. Keep attribution and qualifications beside the public claim when omitting them would mislead. “No reach data” need not become a recurring caption theme unless measurement itself is the reader's question. A named external case stays attributed; a hypothetical workflow stays visibly illustrative. Exact numbers, population, period, uncertainty and required CTA remain protected. When a source names only a general measure, keep that general description. Do not complete its missing question wording, topic or mechanism from domain knowledge or a similar real study. Request the original wording only if the intended claim needs that specificity.

Use plain paragraphs, lists and numbering where they aid reading. Native bold in a long-form article does not establish a bold button in a short feed composer. A third-party styled-text utility is optional and needs its own preview check; keep a plain-text fallback. Verify changing platform features only when the task depends on them.

## Bind the visual to the explanation

Specify what the graphic should help the reader understand or do. Assign visible roles: title/problem, example or steps, emphasis, proof/context and next action. Choose a worksheet, comparison, annotated message, real output, scoped chart or other meaningful form. A photo or scene supplies setting only when it improves understanding. Do not fill space with unrelated images or generic blocks.

Map every numerical/offer claim and fictional label to the actual panel. A concise visual paraphrase can differ from the caption if meaning and material qualifications survive. Preserve the current design system; do not infer it from an old release. Export revision-matched alt text/transcript and inspect actual rendering for clipping, hierarchy and readability. A structural pass is not artistic acceptance or a performance test.

## Automation and human handoff

Agents can prepare source/card retrieval, planning options, hooks, outlines, drafts, fact comparisons, visual briefs and export checks. The author supplies truthful experience, actual stance and product facts, and reviews the final revision. Account changes, publication and outreach remain separate actions. Return the requested copy first; include a proportionate source map, unresolved questions and actual checks separately. Do not imply human approval from another agent's review.
